# Research Round 165 — The neutral channel: choice-only members shouldn't price confidence

Date: 2026-10-16

The B prompt made the LLM tiers **choice-only**: they answer with a choice
and a constant placeholder probability (0.5). The gate's mean-probability
channel, however, cannot tell a placeholder from a measured confidence, so
a neutral member drags every row's mean toward 0.5 - on split F this
declined 21 extra rows for reasons that had nothing to do with
disagreement (round 164).

This round measured what excluding neutral votes from *that channel alone*
(counts and agreement untouched) would do, offline, on the recorded votes:

| suite | auto (current -> neutral) | auto acc | decided advisor | decided veto |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D (4B member) | 224 -> 226 | 0.9375 -> 0.9381 | 0.9333 -> 0.9333 | 0.9375 -> 0.9375 |
| E (8B member) | 234 -> 236 | 0.9957 -> 0.9958 | 0.9833 -> 0.9833 | 0.9875 -> **0.9917** |
| F (8B member, general) | 101 -> 106 | 0.9604 -> 0.9528 | 0.8083 -> 0.8417 | 0.9667 -> 0.9583 |

The effect is small but consistent: more rows auto-execute at essentially
the same precision (F's five extra rows carried four hits), and the veto
fallback's load shrinks accordingly (F veto dips because fewer disputes
remain for the witness; E veto improves). No split gets worse by more than
one row.

The design answer: a vote should be able to say *"I have no confidence to
report"* explicitly, instead of spoofing 0.5. Task 016 (dispatched)
implements exactly that: `neutral` on the vote wire, the OpenAI backend
marks itself neutral by construction, the aggregate excludes neutral votes
from `mean_probability` but keeps them in the counts, and the old behavior
is preserved for every payload that does not set the flag.
