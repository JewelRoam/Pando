# Research Round 99 — The gate works: 92.9% at half coverage

Date: 2026-10-14

## What was measured

Round 98 ended with "knows when it is guessing: partly". This round turns it
into a number by running the three exported lineages (en / ml / td) over the
27 labeled choice tasks, aggregating them exactly as `rootd` does, and
asking how accuracy changes as the gate is allowed to cover less of the
suite (`tools/gating_check.py`, host-only: it needs the 3 graph files).

## Result

```text
overall accuracy   : 51.8%   (14/27)
auto coverage      : 51.8% at 92.9% accuracy
unanimous coverage : 55.6% at 80.0% accuracy
split agreement    : 16.7% accuracy
confidence         : 0.825 when correct vs 0.427 when wrong
```

**Acting only on `tier=auto` covers half the work at 92.9% accuracy against
an undifferentiated 51.8%.** The gate is not decorative; it roughly doubles
the hit rate of the work it lets through, and it is the swarm's own signal —
no external judge, no larger model, no labels at decision time.

Three things follow from the breakdown:

- **Splits are worse than useless.** When the three lineages disagree
  (44.4% of the suite) the majority is right 16.7% of the time - 1 case in
  6. Abstaining on disagreement is not caution, it is the difference between
  52% and 93%. This sharpens round 57b's earlier 36% on a different slice:
  the direction is the same and the effect is large.
- **Confidence separates cleanly.** Correct decisions average 0.825
  confidence, wrong ones 0.427, which is why the `mean_probability >= 0.6`
  clause does work even among unanimous votes: unanimous-but-hesitant cases
  (80.0% accurate) are held while unanimous-and-confident cases (92.9%) are
  acted on.
- **The gate is cheap.** The full suite is 81 forward passes; the gate costs
  one aggregation over votes the swarm already collects.

## What this means for the autonomy loop

Combining round 98 and round 99, the honest capability table is now:

| capability | evidence |
|---|---|
| find its own decisions | **no** - the act head saturates; the agenda must come from outside |
| choose among options | 51.8% overall, 92.9% on what it accepts |
| know when it is guessing | **yes, measured** - the two-part gate takes 51.8% coverage at 92.9% accuracy |
| know when it is alone | no from the tier; requires the responder policy added in round 98 |
| act / refuse to act | yes, and the refusal is what buys the accuracy |

That is a workable division of labour for the goal: the swarm supplies
competence and refusal at a known rate, and the missing piece is an
external agenda. It also means the autonomy loop from round 98 can be
trusted to run unattended *within its coverage*, with everything outside
that coverage held for review - which is exactly what `Journal` records.

## Caveats

- one suite (27 English choice tasks) and one gate configuration
  (`auto_min_agreement 0.6`, `auto_min_probability 0.6`); the curve in
  `metrics/gating-checks/*.json` is the data to tune against, and the
  thresholds were not fitted here;
- `rootd` applies its `Calibrator` before aggregation; this run aggregates
  raw confidences, so the exact tier boundaries in a live swarm can differ
  slightly;
- the suite is small: 14 auto decisions at 92.9% is 13/14, so the interval
  around that number is wide.

## Next step

Sweep `auto_min_agreement` and `auto_min_probability` over the recorded
curve to pick thresholds for a target accuracy (say 90%) at maximum
coverage, then re-run `tools/autonomy_check.py` with those thresholds and
confirm the loop holds the same fraction it did offline.
