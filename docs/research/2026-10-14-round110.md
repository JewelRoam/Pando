# Research Round 110 — A quorum that knows how busy it is

Date: 2026-10-14

## Two follow-ups from round 109

Round 109 showed that the scaling unit is the quorum: growth alone bought 9%,
while a fixed quorum plus rotation bought 1.9x. It left two weaknesses named:
the rotation was *blind* (it spread load without knowing which voters were
busy), and `k` was a static deployment decision.

## Load-aware selection

`Coordinator` now tracks in-flight calls per node, and `pick_quorum` orders
candidates by (in-flight, latency band, rotation). Load comes first, so a
queued voter is not chosen; near-equal latencies fall in the same 25 ms band
so the rotation still spreads concurrent decisions over interchangeable
members.

Six serialized replicas at 50 ms each:

| k | concurrency | round 109 (blind) | round 110 (load-aware) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 4 | 37.4 req/s, p50 102.8 ms | **43.4 req/s, p50 83.6 ms** |
| 2 | 8 | 46.5 req/s, p50 147.3 ms | **57.7 req/s, p50 109.5 ms** |

16% more throughput at concurrency 4 and 24% at 8, with *lower* latency: it
stopped sending work to voters that were already answering a decision.

## Adaptive quorum size

`rootd --adaptive-quorum --latency-target-ms T --quorum-floor F` lets `k`
move between the floor and the ceiling once per decision: it shrinks while
the decision latency EWMA exceeds `T` and recovers below `0.6 * T`. The floor
is a policy decision - how few independent voters are still acceptable - not
a tuning knob.

Same six replicas, target 120 ms, floor 1, ceiling 5:

| concurrency | throughput | p50 | quorum |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 12.4 req/s | 78.2 ms | **5** |
| 4 | 31.9 req/s | 101.4 ms | 5 |
| 8 | **71.2 req/s** | 97.0 ms | **3** |

Compare the static choices at concurrency 8: k=5 gives 23.5 req/s at 306 ms,
k=2 gives 57.7 req/s at 109.5 ms. The adaptive root **held full agreement
while idle** and gave it up only as far as the latency target required,
landing at k=3 and hitting its target (97 ms <= 120 ms) with more throughput
than either static setting in this sweep.

That is the operating point round 109 said the architecture needed: a swarm
that *checks* when it can afford to and *serves* when it cannot.

## Caveats, stated plainly

- one sweep per configuration, 24 decisions per point, and `quorum_size` is
  the last decision's value rather than an average - so the k=3 settling
  point is a snapshot, and the throughput differences at concurrency 8 carry
  real noise;
- the adaptive policy reacts to a 4-decision EWMA, so it lags a step change
  in load and can oscillate around the target;
- the simulated device is a fixed delay, not a queueing model: the *shape*
  of these results is the finding, not the absolute numbers;
- the floor was set to 1 for the experiment. In production it is the same
  knob as "how much agreement am I willing to give up", and round 99's
  result - disagreement is where the errors are - is the reason it should
  rarely be 1.

## Changes

- `Coordinator`: in-flight tracking per node; `pick_quorum` orders by load,
  then latency band, then rotation.
- `Coordinator._adapt_quorum`: per-decision size adjustment against a latency
  target, bounded by a floor and by the live node count.
- `rootd`: `--adaptive-quorum`, `--quorum-floor`, `--latency-target-ms`.
- `tools/scaling_law_check.py`: `--adaptive-quorum/--quorum-floor/
  --latency-target-ms`, and reports labelled so sweeps can be compared.

## Verification

17 smoke tests; `swarm_check`, `slow_stem_check` and `autonomy_check` all
pass with load-aware selection in place, so nothing that depended on the
previous ordering broke.

## Open

- the adaptive policy is a bare proportional controller with one signal. It
  has no notion of *what* the decision is worth: a security question and a
  routing question should not share a floor, and the playbook already
  distinguishes them per category.
- load is counted, not measured: a node that reports "busy" only through
  latency cannot be distinguished from one that is genuinely slow.
- nothing yet retires a replica. The swarm can grow and now tunes its
  quorum, but a device that is slow, idle and expensive stays a member
  forever.
