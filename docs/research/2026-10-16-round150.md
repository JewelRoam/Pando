# Research Round 150 — The project can build the member it was missing

Date: 2026-10-16

## The wall, and the one way through it

Rounds 122-149 closed every lever except one. Rearranging the votes: at most a
point. A narrower ballot: 1.3 points. A second copy of a lineage: nothing.
A correlated family: negative. A better combiner: it earned the oracle but the
oracle was the members' limit. Recalibrating: no transfer. Tuning the gate:
the shipped point was already the grid optimum. Each round ended at the same
place - **the swarm is as good as its members, and nothing in the Laya family
decorrelates from the rest of it**.

The one path the project had not taken to the end is the one it owns: fine-tune
a lineage on its own labels. Rounds 79-81 tried it with 22 contrast pairs and
produced a decision-for-decision copy (round 119 measured that). There are now
**720 labelled rows**, a 3070 with a working CUDA environment, and a training
harness (`tools/rlcd_cuda_train.py`, `laya.train.finetune`).

## Three runs, and only the third one moved

`tools/build_finetune_data.py` writes the 720 rows in the format the trainer
reads, with the contrast rows being the interesting ones: where the recorded
swarm was *wrong* (87 of 720), its answer becomes the `rejected` field, so the
objective is pushed away from the mistake rather than from a random
alternative. Split C was left out entirely - it is the evaluation.

| run | epochs | encoder | accuracy on C | decisions changed vs parent |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | frozen | 0.7292 | **1 / 240** |
| 2 | 3 | frozen | 0.7375 | **3 / 240** |
| 3 | 1 | **unfrozen** | **0.9125** | **62 / 240** |

The frozen runs are rounds 79-81 again, at thirty times the data: training the
decision head alone does not move decisions. Unfreezing the encoder changes
everything - **+17.9 points over its own parent, and 26% divergence from it**.

Verified independently of the training report: the checkpoint was re-run
through the project's own `TorchLayaBackend` on the same split (219/240 =
0.9125 ✓), 0 of the 240 evaluation states appeared in the training set, and
every prediction is a valid candidate. Peak memory 7.9 GB on the 8 GB card;
training took minutes.

## What it does to the swarm

| member | accuracy on C (unseen wording) |
|---|---:|
| `english` | 0.7042 |
| `multilingual` | 0.6083 |
| `typed-decisions` | 0.7375 |
| **`ft-v3`** | **0.9125** |

Its error correlation with the incumbents is **+0.17 to +0.28** - not
independent, but a universe away from round 119's descendant at `phi = +1.000`
and 0% disagreement. It disagrees with each incumbent on 24-40% of rows.

| fleet | majority vote | gate, full coverage | auto-executed | accuracy there |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| the trio | 0.7375 | 0.7708 | 163 | 0.9080 |
| **trio + `ft-v3`** | **0.8042** | **0.8833** | 189 | 0.9153 |

Adding it lifts the gate by **11.25 points at full coverage**, answers 26 more
rows, and is *also* more accurate on the rows it does auto-execute. At matched
coverage (the trio's own 163 rows) the gain is +1.2 points
(0.9080 -> 0.9202) - the honest paired number - and the four-member oracle is
0.9417.

## What this means for the objective

The project's premise is a swarm that grows by reproducing itself. Until today
the measured answer to "does growth make it smarter?" was no: a copy is worth
nothing, and the family could not produce anything else. The answer now is:

- **the swarm can manufacture the member it lacks, from its own labels, on one
  consumer GPU in minutes**;
- that member is **17.5 points better than the best member it had** and
  disagrees with its parent on a quarter of the rows;
- and the fleet it joins improves by **11.25 points**.

This is the first evidence that the loop closes: labels accumulate, a member
is trained, the swarm gets better. Rounds 122-149 built the machinery that
makes such a member *usable* (admission on independence, cost-aware
scheduling, an advisory combiner for the rows the gate declines); this round
shows the ingredient existed all along in the data.

## Caveats, stated before anyone builds on it

**Its confidences are saturated.** Median 1.000, 228 of 240 rows at 1.0. The
trainer fits a temperature, but the exported confidences are still pinned, so
the gate's `mean_probability` threshold is trivially satisfied and a
confidence-weighted vote would be dominated by it. It needs its own
calibration pass before it is trusted in production - and rounds 148-149 say
that pass must be measured, not assumed.

**One split, one task family.** The evaluation is 240 rows of the same
generator with disjoint phrasings. That is a real generalisation test across
wording; it is not evidence about a different task.

**The gate's thresholds were tuned for weaker members.** Round 149 established
that thresholds and confidences are one system; a 91%-accurate member changes
the operating point, so admitting it is not just an admission decision.
