# Research Round 151 — The system, with the member it trained itself

Date: 2026-10-16

## What round 150 left unmeasured

Round 150 trained `ft-v3` on 720 of the project's own labels and measured the
*gate*: 0.7708 -> 0.8833 at full coverage, with 62 of 240 decisions changed
against its parent and an error correlation of +0.17 to +0.28 with the
incumbents. What it did not measure is what the **system** answers - the
gate's choice where it auto-executes plus the advisory combiner's suggestion
everywhere else - which is the number a user sees.

`tools/improved_fleet_check.py` measures it on split C, offline against the
recorded votes (the combiner's embedded text model was trained on wording set
B, so its features are out-of-sample for C like everything else).

## The fleet, and the system

| fleet | gate, full coverage | auto-executed | accuracy there | **decided** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| the trio | 0.7708 | 163 | 0.9080 | 0.7708 |
| trio + shipped combiner | 0.7708 | 163 | 0.9080 | 0.8333 |
| trio + `ft-v3` | 0.8833 | 189 | 0.9153 | 0.8833 |
| **trio + `ft-v3` + shipped combiner** | **0.8833** | 189 | 0.9153 | **0.9083** |

**The system now decides 90.83% of unseen wording**, against 83.33% before the
new member and 77.08% for the bare trio. The gate is both more accurate and
more willing: it auto-executes 26 more rows and is right on 91.53% of them.

## The combiner did not need refitting, and that is the interesting part

Spec 3 made the combiner's features membership-agnostic in round 144, for a
deployment reason: a file that encodes "which named member dissented" cannot
serve a fleet whose members changed. The new member is exactly that case, and
the question was whether a combiner fitted on the *trio* still helps a
*four*-member fleet. Refitting it for the new fleet, on half the rows and
scoring the other half:

| fleet, on 120 rows it was not fitted on | gate | decided |
|---|---:|---:|
| trio + **shipped** combiner | 0.7750 | **0.8333** |
| trio + refitted combiner | 0.7750 | 0.7333 |
| trio + `ft-v3` + **shipped** combiner | 0.8833 | **0.8917** |
| trio + `ft-v3` + refitted combiner | 0.8833 | 0.8083 |

The shipped file transfers to a fleet it has never seen and beats a refit on
half the data by 8-10 points. 120 rows is simply not enough to fit 35
features, which is the same lesson as rounds 137 and 141 arriving from a new
direction: **the combiner wants more labelled rows, not more fitting** - and
the shipped artifact is already the better of the two available answers.

## The session's arc, in four numbers

| round | what was measured | ensemble vs its best member |
|---|---|---:|
| 117 | 27 hand-built tasks | **worse** (51.9% vs 59.3%) |
| 118 | 480 generated tasks | +1.0 point, inside noise |
| 144 | the smallest real fleet (iGPU + a 19 KB member) | 77.5% decided |
| **151** | **the fleet with the member the project trained** | **90.83% decided** |

The three rounds that came between were the work: admission on independence,
one opinion per correlated family, cost-aware scheduling, an advisory combiner
for the rows the gate declines - and, at the end, the realisation that the
missing ingredient was not a rule but a member, and that the project could
grow one from its own data.

## Caveats carried forward

`ft-v3`'s confidences are saturated at 1.0 (228 of 240 rows), so the gate's
probability channel does nothing for it - the numbers above are what the
system does *with* that defect, not in spite of it, and rounds 148-149 say
fixing it will move the operating point in a direction that has to be
measured. The evaluation is one task family with disjoint phrasings. And the
gate's thresholds were tuned for weaker members.
