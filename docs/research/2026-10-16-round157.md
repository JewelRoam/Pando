# Research Round 157 — The Mac joins as one group; and a direction: model tiers, not voter herds

Date: 2026-10-16

## Part 1 — the first member outside the family, the OS, and the architecture

The Mac experiment node (Apple silicon, 16 GB, LAN 192.168.31.41:8765) is
online and attached the way it was specified: it keeps its own root, the
Windows side runs the bridge (Windows -> Mac, never the reverse), and the
whole group registers with the Windows root as **one HTTP stem** with
checkpoint `text-nb-v1` - not a Laya replica, and not a second coordinator.

Verified live:

| step | result |
|---|---|
| `/pairing` | `{"required": true}`, no token needed |
| sample `/decide` (out-of-scope question) | `choice=fix`, agreement 1.0, **mean_probability 0.0**, tier verify, fanout 1.9 ms |
| Windows root (playbook admits `english` + `text-nb-v1`) | both members registered: `win-igpu` (english) and `mac-group` (text-nb-v1) |
| bridge end to end on the sample | 122.9 ms, vote `text-nb-v1 / fix / 0.0` |
| 24 held-out rows through the root (k=2) | mac-group's vote present in **24/24**; tiers auto 6 / verify 18; decided 0.7500; p50 68 ms |

Three measurements worth keeping, beyond "the cable works":

**A foreign confidence scale is declarable.** The Mac's text model refuses
out-of-scope questions with probability 0.0 - the parent gate then holds the
whole decision at `verify` rather than averaging a refusal into a fake
confidence. That is the right first behavior, and it is the first live
instance of the model-combination question below.

**In-family, unseen wording exposes overconfidence, not weakness.** On the 24
held-out rows the Mac answers solo at 0.4167 accuracy while reporting a median
probability of 0.83. Naive Bayes out of distribution is confidently wrong;
the useful signal in its votes is *disagreement*, not confidence. The
aggregation layer must calibrate or down-weight foreign confidences - it
already applies `assets/calibration/text-nb-v1.json` (T=0.8), which is far
too soft to fix a gap this size.

**The bridge is cheap.** 122.9 ms through the bridge process against 1.9 ms
of Mac compute: cross-LAN cost is noise next to any real model's inference
time. Transport will not be the constraint on this topology; capacity and
model quality will.

## Part 2 — the direction: tiers of models, not herds of voters

The critique this round opened with is largely correct, and the session's own
numbers say so:

- The largest jump ever measured here came from a **stronger model**, not
  from more voters: the unfrozen fine-tune moved 0.7333 -> 0.9125 on unseen
  wording (round 150), and the four-member system reached 0.9750 (round 154).
- Replica voters bought *nothing* and then cost latency (rounds 146, 155).
- Weak-but-decorrelated voters (the text model) add marginal information and
  can actively mislead through confidence (this round, and rounds 122-125:
  outsiders collapse on unseen wording).

So the research program should change shape, not restart. What the previous
rounds built is the part that matters for combining *models* and not just
Laya copies, and it is model-agnostic already:

- the wire contract (any process that can answer `choice + probability` is a
  member - this round proved it across OS and model family);
- the gate (auto/verify/escalate - abstention is representable; a refusal at
  0.0 holds the line);
- per-checkpoint calibration and opinion counting (one lineage, one vote);
- the advisory combiner and the measured exchange rates (distinct strong
  opinions up; replicas down; a seat is a purchase).

**The tiering hypothesis to test next, stated so it can fail:** each device
should run the *strongest model it can host*, and the aggregate should
compose across tiers - not make every device carry the same floor model. The
floor model (Laya here) exists to give under-resourced devices *any* vote;
its value is presence, not intelligence. The 16 GB Mac can host real
quantized LLMs (4-8B class via llama.cpp/MLX); the Windows machine has the
3070 for Laya-scale models and the NPU/iGPU for floor work. That is three
tiers on two machines, and the question "which intelligence is optimal for a
given hardware" becomes a placement policy over those tiers rather than a
single-model choice.

What would falsify it: if the Mac's LLM tier, added as a distinct opinion,
does not move the combined system above the best single tier on held-out
wording - or if its confidence semantics poison the gate the way a naive
0.83 median did this round - then the composition layer, not the model tier,
is the bottleneck, and rounds 139-147's combiner machinery gets its real
test.
