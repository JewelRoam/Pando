# Research Round 164 — The breadth axis: the witness flips with the family

Date: 2026-10-16

Every evaluation so far lived in one intent family (8 intents, wording A-F
variants). Task 013 built a suite from **six brand-new intents** (F, 120
rows: `big7-*`, billing_clarify / content_appeal / data_export /
device_triage / permission_escalation / reschedule) and the LLM tiers voted
on it zero-shot. This round added the Laya tiers' votes and measured the
whole story.

## Solo on F (zero-shot; nothing below trained on these intents)

| member | F | (old-family means, D/E) |
|---|---:|---|
| english | 0.8417 | 0.7834 |
| ft-v3 | 0.8667 | 0.9667 |
| mac-llm (4B) | 0.9333 | 0.8667 |
| **mac-llm-8b** | **0.9833** | 0.8917 |

The hypothesis going in - "the Laya tiers collapse outside their family" -
was **too strong**: they degrade gracefully (0.84-0.87), keeping their
decision machinery useful on 3-4-way choices. The LLM tiers still lead
(+11.6 points for the 8B), so breadth is real, but as a gradient, not a
cliff. (Caveat: F may be easier than intended; its candidates are
well-separated.)

## Composition on F

| configuration | auto | auto acc | decided |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| english + ft-v3 (Laya core) | 97 | 0.9485 | 0.7667 |
| core + 8B, no assent | 99 | 0.9495 | 0.7833 |
| core + 8B, assent=ft-v3 (veto) | 99 | 0.9495 | 0.8667 |
| **core + 8B, assent=8B (veto)** | 94 | **0.9894** | **0.9833** |

Three findings that change the design:

**The best witness is per-family.** ft-v3 is the trusted member on the old
family (means 0.967) but answers only 0.8667 on F; the 8B - mediocre on the
old family (0.892) - is the right witness on F (0.983). `assent=auto`
resolving by a *global* mean picks the wrong witness for out-of-family
work; the registry needs a family dimension.

**The advisor does not transfer at all.** On F the combiner's suggestions
scored 0/23 on the declined rows - an out-of-family fitted rule is worse
than useless; the veto fallback is what carried the system (0.7667 ->
0.9833 as the witness improves).

**Composition reaches the strongest tier's ceiling, with a precision
premium.** With the 8B as witness: auto 94 @ 0.9894 (99% precision at 78%
coverage) and decided 0.9833 - the fleet decides at its best member's
accuracy while *refusing* the rows where that member is unsure.

### Update — family-aware assent merged, and the live F gap root-caused

Task 014 landed registry v2 + family-aware `assent=auto` (Task 014's chain
again needed a three-way merge against its own older base; tests came
through cleanly, one rootd conflict resolved by hand; 27/27 smoke on
Windows). Live on F (`--family general --assent-checkpoint auto
--assent-fallback veto`, members english + ft-v3 + 8B):

- `assent=mac-llm-8b, assent_family=general` on **120/120 rows** - the
  family-resolution works on hardware;
- auto **94 @ 0.9894** - matching the offline matrix to the digit;
- decided **0.8083**, not the offline 0.9833. The per-row log shows why:
  only 5 rows are assent-demoted (veto answered 4), while **21 further
  declined rows** - 13 stopped solely because the constant p=0.5 member
  drags `mean_probability` under the auto bar, 8 with no suggestion -
  fell to the out-of-family advisor (0/13) instead of the witness. The
  offline matrix had implicitly let the witness answer *every* declined
  row; the live root only let it answer its own dissent rows.

Task 015 (dispatched) extends `veto` to "the witness answers whenever the
gate does not auto-execute", which is both the simpler semantic and the one
the offline numbers assumed. It also surfaced a standing minor question:
should a choice-only member (constant p=0.5) be allowed to drag the gate's
mean-probability channel at all?
