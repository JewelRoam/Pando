# Research Round 128 — Scaling is a membership problem, not a head-count

Date: 2026-10-15

## The question the elastic premise lives on

Round 126 shipped one cheap independent member and round 127 made it pay
(+1.25 points). The premise is elastic: *more* devices should mean *more*
intelligence. This round asks what the second, third and fourth additions are
worth, and separates "too few members" from "too weak members" from "too
similar members".

## Real members: the first one pays, the rest do not

Four cheap members - the shipped word-and-bigram naive Bayes, a character
trigram naive Bayes, both together, and a nearest-centroid classifier - added
to the recorded three-lineage swarm on the 240-row wording-held-out split.
Paired bootstrap, 2000 resamples, against the trio at 77.08%:

| portfolio | full coverage | change vs trio | better in |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| trio alone | 77.08% | - | - |
| + `centroid` (42.9% accurate) | **80.42%** | **+3.39** | **100.0%** |
| + shipped words | 78.33% | +1.33 | 70.9% |
| + mixed | 77.50% | +0.50 | 53.8% |
| + chars | 76.67% | -0.33 | 40.7% |
| + words + centroid | 78.75% | +1.74 | 73.5% |
| + words + chars + centroid | 70.00% | -7.00 | 1.4% |
| + all four | 65.42% | **-11.62** | 0.1% |

The best single addition is the **weakest member tested** (42.9% accurate),
and it is the only one whose gain is unambiguous: +3.39 points, ahead in
every one of 2000 resamples. The fourth addition is not merely unhelpful - it
is a large, statistically solid loss.

Two reweightings of the choice rule - by calibrated confidence mass, and by
each lineage's accuracy on a labeled probe set - do not change this. Both
leave the cliff where it is (all four: 64.58% and 65.00% against 65.42%),
which rules out "the count is being stolen by weak voters" as the whole
story.

## Why: the additions resemble each other

Pairwise phi over every member, on the same rows:

| | chars | mixed | centroid | trio (each) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| shipped words | +0.541 | +0.636 | +0.687 | -0.06 to +0.12 |
| chars | - | **+0.898** | +0.467 | -0.13 to +0.26 |
| mixed | | - | +0.541 | -0.14 to +0.24 |

Every cheap member is *decorrelated from the neural trio* and *strongly
correlated with the other cheap members*. The first one buys the family's
information; the rest are near-copies of it. This is exactly the pattern the
project has now found three times - prompt variants of one checkpoint (round
118), a fine-tuned descendant (round 119), a quantised sibling (round 125) -
and it is the same pattern, not a coincidence of any one of them.

## Synthetic control: independence alone scales, at any competence

Real members cannot separate count from competence: there are four cheap
ones and their accuracies are close. So the same recorded trio was given k
synthetic voters, right with probability p and otherwise uniformly wrong,
with no correlation to the trio or to each other, averaged over 30
independent draws. Full-coverage accuracy:

| added accuracy | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.50 | 77.1% | 79.0% | 80.7% | 84.1% | 86.3% | **90.4%** |
| 0.60 | 77.1% | 80.2% | 82.8% | 86.7% | 89.6% | **94.2%** |
| 0.70 | 77.1% | 81.6% | 85.1% | 89.5% | 92.7% | **96.8%** |
| 0.80 | 77.1% | 83.0% | 87.3% | 91.7% | 95.6% | **98.9%** |
| 0.90 | 77.1% | 84.3% | 88.9% | 93.9% | 97.8% | **99.8%** |

Monotone in count at every accuracy, including coin-flip members. The
mechanism is the old one: a wrong vote scatters over the other seven options,
so a weak but independent voter mostly adds a vote to the right answer when
it is right and almost never builds a wrong majority. Six 50% members are
worth **+13 points**; six 60% members are worth +17.

## What this settles

The two measurements bracket the elastic premise:

- **if the additions are independent**, the swarm scales with count, and it
  does not need strong members - 50% voters are enough to gain, 60% voters
  are worth +17 points at six of them;
- **if the additions resemble what is already seated**, more devices are
  worth nothing and eventually cost accuracy, no matter how the votes are
  combined.

The project's own fleet is in the second regime for a reason that has now
been measured from four directions: everything it can produce from one
family is correlated, and so is every cheap re-reading of the same text. A
heterogeneous fleet is worth building, but the heterogeneity that pays is in
*error structure*, not in bit-width, prompt, checkpoint or model size - and
the number of devices is worth nothing without it.

The practical rule: a candidate needs a seat only if its divergence from the
members already seated clears a threshold (the phi column above), and once a
family is seated, further members of that family are capacity, not
intelligence.
