# Research Round 139 — A learned combiner earns part of the gap, and locates the rest

Date: 2026-10-15

## The gap rounds 118 and 120 could not close

Round 118 located the swarm's headroom precisely: on the rows where the three
lineages disagree, the correct answer is present in the trio 91.7% of the
time, and the best *hand-written* rule over the same votes captures 74.5%.
Round 120 tried narrowing the ballot instead and got 1.3 points. Every
combiner tested in this project so far has been a rule a human wrote -
majority, weighted vote, unanimity-first, most- or least-confident member.

This round fits one instead.

## The design

`tools/learned_combiner_check.py` treats the quorum's votes as the *features*
and the label as the target: per candidate, how many members chose it and how
much calibrated confidence stands behind it; the size of the majority; which
members dissent; what each member believed. Twenty-two features, a multinomial
logistic regression trained by gradient descent, regularisation chosen by
five-fold cross-validation **on the dev split alone**, then evaluated once on
the 240 wording-held-out rows - no wording and no row shared with the fit.

## The result

| group | rows | learned combiner | best single member | shipped gate | oracle |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| the gate's own rows | 172 | **90.70%** | 88.95% | 90.12% | 91.28% |
| the rows the gate declines | 68 | **54.41%** | 41.18% | - | 67.65% |
| all rows | 240 | **80.42%** | 75.42% | 77.08% | 84.58% |

Three things follow.

**The swarm beats its best member for the first time by a clear margin**: 80.4%
against 75.4% at full coverage, +5.0 points, where rounds 118-137 measured an
ensemble edge of about one point. What the fit adds is not a better choice on
the rows the swarm already answers - there it is 0.6 points from the oracle
(90.70 against 91.28) - but a decision on the rows the swarm *declines*.

**The headroom now has an address.** The gate's rules are essentially at the
ceiling on the rows they accept, and the remaining 7 points of oracle sit in
the 68 rows it refuses. A learned combiner reaches **54.4%** of them against
the best member's 41.2% and an oracle of 67.7% - it captures roughly
three-quarters of the available headroom in exactly the place rounds 118-120
could only describe.

**More training rows do not help here.** Pooling the second dev report (480
rows instead of 240 - legitimate, because the features are vote patterns, not
text) leaves the held-out result unchanged (79.6% against 80.4%, inside
noise) and the matched-coverage figure identical. At this size the limit is
the members and the features, not the number of labelled rows.

## What this changes

A fitted combiner is an artifact like a calibration table: it is trained on
labelled rows the project already accumulates, it is a few hundred numbers,
and it ships with the playbook. The gate's hand-written thresholds have served
the project well - they are safe, explainable and within 0.6 points of the
oracle on the rows they accept - and the fitted rule is what decides the rows
where safety says "I do not know".

Two caveats, stated because they bound the claim: at the gate's own coverage
the learned rule is ahead by **one decision** (90.70 against 90.12), which is
not a separation; and the fit needs labels, so it belongs to the design side
of the swarm rather than to the device side - a device still votes with the
rules it was shipped with.
