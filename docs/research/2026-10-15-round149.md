# Research Round 149 — The shipped gate is at the optimum of its own grid

Date: 2026-10-15

## The procedure round 148 implied

Round 148 showed calibration and gating are one system: refitting temperatures
on more rows made the confidences sharper, which satisfied
`auto_min_probability` more often, which made the gate auto-execute more rows
at lower accuracy. The obvious consequence was a tuning procedure - fit the
temperatures *and* the thresholds on a deployment's own labelled decisions,
then use them - and a practical question: how many of its own rows does a
deployment need before that beats what it shipped?

`tools/tune_gate_check.py` measures it. The 240 rows of split C are cut into a
tuning half and an evaluation half; the temperatures and both gate thresholds
are chosen on the tuning half alone; the coverage and accuracy are reported on
the evaluation half.

## The answer is no, and it is not close

| own rows used to tune | auto-executed (evaluation half) | accuracy there |
|---:|---:|---:|
| **shipped tables and thresholds** | **83** | **0.9036** |
| 25 | 95 | 0.8632 |
| 50 | 87 | 0.8966 |
| 100 | 75 | 0.9200 |

At a 90% accuracy floor the shipped configuration accepts 83 rows; the best
tuned configuration accepts 75 and buys 1.6 points of accuracy with them. With
25 or 50 of its own rows the tuning does not even hold the floor on rows it
did not see.

## Why: the shipped configuration is the grid optimum

The honest way to close this was to let the tuning *peek*: sweep the same
threshold grid on the evaluation half itself, which is an upper bound no
deployment can reach.

| | auto-executed | accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|
| shipped (0.6, 0.6) | 83 | 0.9036 |
| **in-sample ceiling** (thresholds chosen on the evaluation half) | **83** | **0.9036** |

They are the same number. Within this grid, on this distribution, at a 90%
floor, **no threshold choice beats the shipped one** - so no amount of
deployment data and no tuning procedure could have won this round. Both of
this round's attempts (round 148's calibration refit and this one's threshold
tuning) failed against the same wall, and the second measurement is what turns
"it did not work" into "it could not have worked here".

## The fragility this leaves behind

The optimum is real but it is not *designed*. Round 148 measured that
`typed-decisions`' shipped temperature is five times softer than its own 240
rows want, and that correcting it makes the gate auto-execute 16 more rows at
four points lower accuracy. In other words: **part of the shipped gate's
safety comes from a calibration error acting as a margin.**

That is worth writing down where the next person will look. The playbook
example now says it beside the thresholds: recalibrating a lineage without
re-tuning `auto_min_gating` moves the operating point, and moving it
*correctly* was not achievable on the data available here.
