# Research Round 138 — What one more device costs, and what it buys

Date: 2026-10-15

## The missing number

The objective's third clause is reproduction, and the project has measured
its parts in isolation: a whole artifact crosses the wire at 1.68 GB in
14.2 s (round 106) or 105 MB as a delta (round 107), a replica counts as one
opinion rather than a second one (round 121), and units of one lineage on one
host add nothing beyond the second (rounds 132-133). Never measured was the
end-to-end number a reproduction budget needs: **how long from "a device
decides to join" until the fleet's quorum hears its first vote.**

`tools/device_birth_check.py` measures it against a running root - the
incumbent joins, the fleet is driven under load, a newcomer loads the
artifact, joins, is forced into the quorum (k=2) until it votes, and the
fleet is driven again.

## The balance sheet

| leg | measured | source |
|---|---:|---|
| transfer, whole artifact (1.69 GB) | 14.2 s | round 106 (not re-measured here, see below) |
| transfer, delta | 105 MB | round 107 |
| model load | **9.4 s** | this round |
| register + first heartbeat | **0.2 s** | this round |
| first vote (warmup included) | **5.0 s** | this round |
| **birth, transfer excluded** | **14.6 s** | this round |
| **birth, whole artifact** | **~29 s** | composed |

and what the newborn buys:

| fleet | throughput | p50 | served by |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| one member (k=1) | 26.1 /s | 76 ms | incumbent x30 |
| two members (k=2) | **13.5 /s** | 143 ms | incumbent 26, newborn 4 |

**Reproduction costs about half a minute and buys nothing.** The second
member of the same lineage is the same opinion (round 121), on the same
silicon (round 133), and putting it in the quorum only adds its latency to
every decision (round 132) - throughput halves when k goes from 1 to 2,
exactly as the earlier rounds predict. The newborn's four votes are the
quorum working, not the fleet improving.

## Why the transfer leg is cited rather than re-measured

`pull_spore` refuses to run without `sandbox_confirmed=True`, and that gate is
the threat model's answer to a real question: a device that pulls and then
executes a lineage from the network has to be one the operator is willing to
sandbox. This check does not assert that gate on its own authority - the
newborn here is an ordinary local process, not a verified container or VM -
so the transfer leg stays with the tools that own it (rounds 106-108) and
this round measures everything after it. The refusal is the correct
behaviour, and it is worth noting that it fires on the *check tool* rather
than on the library.

## What this closes, and what it opens

Together with rounds 121, 129 and 137, the economics of a growing swarm are
now measured end to end:

- **cost of one more device**: ~29 s of wall clock and one artifact transfer;
- **value of one more device**: zero unless it brings an opinion the swarm
  does not already hold - which the same rounds showed cannot be manufactured
  from the family the swarm is made of;
- **the only lever that pays**: admitting members whose errors are
  independent, and even then only the first of a family (round 128).

So a self-replicating swarm is cheap to grow and hard to make smarter, and
the two halves of that sentence are now both numbers rather than
expectations. The next useful work is not faster reproduction - it is finding
one genuinely independent opinion that a device can hold.
