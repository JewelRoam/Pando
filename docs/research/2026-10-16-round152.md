# Research Round 152 — The loop works; one run of it is not a measurement

Date: 2026-10-16

## What round 150 left as a single point

Round 150 fine-tuned `english` on 720 of the project's own labels and measured
0.9125 on an unseen wording split against its parent's 0.7333. Round 151
measured what the system does with it (0.9083 decided). Both were **one run
each** - one sample of a procedure the project now intends to run again, and
again.

So this round did the obvious next thing twice over: a fourth wording
inventory (`--cues d`, 0 of 48 cues overlapping A, A2, B or C) so that the
evaluation never doubles as training data, the same recipe on a **larger
corpus** (960 rows: the 720 plus split C), and **replication** - five
fine-tunes across the two corpora, every one of them scored on the same
untouched split.

## The curve, with its spread

All numbers are accuracy on split D (240 rows, seen by nothing that follows):

| corpus (rows the trainer kept) | samples | mean | range | spread |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 0 (the parent) | 1 | 0.7458 | - | - |
| 648 (of 720) | 3 | **0.9292** | 0.9125 - 0.9625 | 5.0 |
| 864 (of 960) | 2 | 0.8959 | 0.8292 - 0.9625 | **13.3** |

Two findings, one robust and one deflationary.

**The loop's first step is unambiguous.** Every sample of every corpus sits 17
to 22 points above the parent. Training on the project's own labels is not a
hopeful idea; it is the largest single improvement measured in this entire
session, and it reproduces.

**The second step is not measurable, and one run of anything here is not a
measurement.** The two corpora's ranges overlap; the larger corpus's own
spread (13.3 points) is four times the difference between the means (3.3).
The five runs differ only in corpus and seed, and their spread - 5 to 13
points - is comparable to the *entire* gain the loop is chasing between
generations.

## What that means for a swarm that intends to keep doing this

Rounds 122-149 built an admission rule on *independence* (does the candidate
disagree with the members already seated?) and round 150 added *capability*
(is the candidate better on a split none of it saw?). This round adds the
third condition, and it is the one a self-improving system will violate by
default: **replication**. A fine-tune that scored 0.83 may be the same recipe
as one that scored 0.96; admitting on a single run is admitting on a draw.

The cheap procedure that follows is what the project's own tools already
support - train k seeds, score each on a fresh split, admit the best only if
it is outside the spread of what is already seated. The measurement above is
the calibration for "outside the spread": at this corpus size, that is five
to thirteen points.
