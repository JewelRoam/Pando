# Research Round 127 — A tied quorum should answer, not go silent

Date: 2026-10-15

## The rule that broke when the swarm grew

`aggregate` treated a tied count as no decision: with half the votes on one
choice and half on another, it returned `choice=None` at the `verify` tier.
That is defensible for three voters, where a tie is impossible, and it was
the behaviour the smoke suite pinned. Round 126 gave the swarm a fourth
member and the rule turned against it: a weak, independent member turns some
2-1 disagreements into 2-2 splits, and every one of those became a
non-decision. Accepted coverage fell 172 rows to 154, full-coverage accuracy
fell 73.75% to 72.92%, and a member that round 123 measured as worth several
points became a small loss.

The count was throwing away information it had. When two choices have equal
vote counts, their confidences need not be equal - and round 118 had already
measured, on 157 disagreements, that the confidence-weighted rule beats a
plain count (74.5% against 70.7%).

## The change

`pandosi/common.py`: when the top two choices tie on count, the quorum now
ranks them by total confidence mass and returns the heavier one at the
`verify` tier. The safety property is untouched - a tie can never reach
`auto`, because `agreement` is still the tied share (0.5) and the auto
threshold is 0.6. A *genuine* tie, where the masses are equal too, still
returns no choice, which is what the existing smoke case asserts and what
this change deliberately preserves.

## Measured, on the wording-held-out split, three rounds of recorded votes

| configuration | full coverage | auto-accepted | accuracy where auto | rows answered |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| trio, before | 73.75% | 172 | 90.12% | 224 |
| trio, shipped | **77.08%** | 172 | 90.12% | 240 |
| + text member, before | 72.92% | 154 | 90.91% | 213 |
| + text member, shipped | **78.33%** | 154 | 90.91% | 240 |

Four things follow from the table:

- the rule is worth **3.3 points** to the three-member swarm on its own,
  because a 3-way tie is also a tie;
- it is worth **5.4 points** to the four-member swarm;
- the independent member is now a *gain* (77.08% -> 78.33%) instead of a
  loss, which is round 123's prediction recovered on the live path;
- the `auto` tier is untouched in both rows - same count, same accuracy - so
  nothing that could execute changed. The gain is entirely in what the swarm
  now *says* about rows it refuses to act on.

## What this says about growing a swarm

A quorum's aggregation rule is not scale-free. Every change to membership
size changes the arithmetic of agreement: three voters give 1.0 / 0.667 /
0.333, four give 1.0 / 0.75 / 0.5, and the shipped 0.6 threshold silently
moves meaning with them. An elastic swarm adds and removes members
continuously, so this class of defect is not a one-off - it is what happens
whenever the membership changes and the rule does not. The check for it is
cheap: replay a recorded run's votes through the aggregator at the new
membership size and compare, which is exactly what
`tools/tie_break_check.py` does and what round 126's live run made necessary.
