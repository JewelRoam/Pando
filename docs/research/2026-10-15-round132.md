# Research Round 132 — Corroboration is paid for in throughput

Date: 2026-10-15

## What the three units can be asked

Rounds 130-131 verified that the CPU, the Intel iGPU and the Intel NPU answer
identically on every task (240/240, confidence gaps of 0.0002 and 0.0003), at
17.6, 10.0 and 8.7 decisions per second. Because the answers are identical,
the fleet can be measured for capacity alone: every difference between
configurations is *only* capacity, with no accuracy question entangled.

`tools/hetero_capacity_check.py` runs a matrix - how many units the fleet
holds, and how many of them one decision waits for - under four concurrent
clients, 160 decisions per cell:

| configuration | throughput | p50 | p95 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 unit, k=1 | 14.7 /s | 262 ms | 383 ms |
| 2 units, k=1 | **27.1 /s** | 133 ms | 280 ms |
| 3 units, k=1 | 24.1 /s | 140 ms | 266 ms |
| 2 units, k=2 | 13.3 /s | 300 ms | 390 ms |
| 3 units, k=2 | 14.4 /s | 245 ms | 491 ms |
| 3 units, k=3 | 10.5 /s | 374 ms | 500 ms |

## Two laws, and one of them was a surprise

**Throughput falls as the quorum grows.** At three units: k=1 24.1/s, k=2
14.4/s, k=3 10.5/s, with p50 latency going 140 -> 245 -> 374 ms. A decision
waits for the members it picked, so every voter added to the quorum adds its
own latency to every decision. Corroboration is not free and it is not paid
in accuracy - it is paid in throughput, roughly in proportion to k.

**Units buy capacity only when a decision goes to one of them.** With k=1 the
two units reach 27.1/s against 14.7/s for the CPU alone, and 10.0 + 17.6 =
27.6/s is exactly the sum of the two units measured in isolation. The load
ordering in `pick_quorum` spreads work across them, and their capacities add.

**But the third unit did not add capacity.** 3 units at k=1 measured 24.1/s
in this run and 30.8/s in an earlier one - run-to-run variation of 25% around
the sum that would be expected (10.0 + 17.6 + 8.7 = 36.3/s). The honest
reading is that the NPU contributes little and sometimes subtracts, and the
reason is structural: **these are not three independent servers.** All three
stems run on one host, and the host CPU does the tokenization, the data
movement and the orchestration for the GPU and NPU stems as well as running
the CPU stem. Three "compute units" on one machine share one scheduler, one
memory bus and one Python process's worth of work per request.

That is the difference between *heterogeneous execution* and *heterogeneous
capacity*. The units are interchangeable where correctness is concerned
(round 131) and their raw rates differ by 2x, but on a single host they are
not additive beyond two of them.

## What this says about elastic scaling

The project's adaptive quorum (rounds 110-112) trades voters for latency
under load. This round measures the exchange rate on real silicon: at three
units, dropping from k=3 to k=1 multiplies throughput by 2.3 and halves p50
latency, and the decision's *answer* is unchanged because the members are
copies of one lineage. Corroboration is a dial with a price, and an elastic
fleet should treat it as one - which is exactly what the latency target and
the quorum floor already do, now with numbers behind the setting.

It also sharpens where the project's real scaling story lies. Growing a
single machine's unit count runs into the host; growing *across* machines is
the case where the units stop sharing a scheduler, and that is the case the
project has demonstrated only with mock stems so far (round 124).
