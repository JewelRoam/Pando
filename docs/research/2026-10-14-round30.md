# Research Round 30 — backend-numerics diversity REFUTED; lazy-compile cliff (2026-10-14)

## Experiment
Same lineage (english fp32), same 40 tasks, two backends:
ORT-CPU vs OpenVINO-iGPU (surgery graph, lazy per-shape compile).

## Result 1: backend numerics do NOT diverge (honest refutation)
- divergent decisions: 0/40 (divergence_rate 0.0)
- mean abs confidence diff: 0.0005
- Round 14 hypothesized GPU-vs-CPU floating-point paths would add natural
  decision diversity. REFUTED: fp32 paths are numerically near-identical.
  Checkpoint diversity remains the only demonstrated decision-diversity
  axis (plus future temperature/schema variants, untested).

## Result 2: lazy per-shape compile is a cliff for varied-length workloads
- 40 tasks -> ~40 distinct seq lengths -> ~40 GPU compiles x ~8s
- GPU p50 across the suite: 7883 ms (vs 25.6 ms steady-state single-shape)
- Fix options (documented, not yet implemented):
  a) pad inputs to fixed-shape buckets in the OV shim (needs pad-token
     semantics from the tokenizer - doable, medium effort)
  b) OV dynamic-shape GPU support (upstream)
  c) route only fixed-length workloads to the GPU EU

## Consequences for PandoSI
- Quorum diversity must come from checkpoint/schema/temperature axes, NOT
  from backend numerics. Round-2 literature requirement stands unchanged.
- iGPU EU in the current shape is best for FIXED-SHAPE workloads (single
  question template with padded inputs); variable workloads need fix (a).
- tools/backend_compare.py committed as the reusable divergence measurer.
