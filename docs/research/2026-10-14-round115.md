# Research Round 115 — Growth that knows where it is going, and undoes its own mistakes

Date: 2026-10-14

## The result round 114 left behind

Round 114 wired growth to real reproduction and then measured something
uncomfortable: the first reproduction took p50 from 449.7 ms to 119.3 ms, and
the second - another ONNX worker on the *same host* - took it to 981 ms. The
swarm had made things worse and had no way to undo it.

Two inputs were missing: mechanical knowledge of *where* a member would live,
and a way to reverse a change that did not pay off.

## Host awareness

`Member` now carries the host its registration URL names, and the supervisor
refuses to grow a lineage onto a machine that already runs one:

```text
round 3: hold: 95 ms is slow, but 127.0.0.1/english already carries 1
         member(s) of this lineage: another would share its compute and add
         no information (rounds 95, 114)
```

The justification is two measurements, not a heuristic: round 95 showed a
runtime and a replica do not change a vote, and round 114 showed a second
same-lineage worker on one host costing 7x in p50. `max_per_host` defaults to
1 and is raisable when an operator wants redundancy more than throughput.

Same setup as round 114, with the rule in place:

| run | reproductions | final p50 |
|---|---:|---:|
| round 114, no host awareness | 2 | 791.8 ms |
| round 115, host-aware | 1 | **126.5 ms** |

One reproduction, 363.0 -> 126.5 ms, and no degradation phase at all.

## Rollback

Host awareness prevents the mistake it knows about. For the ones it does not,
the supervisor now treats a growth as a bet: it records the decision latency
before the growth and which node the growth produced, and after the cooldown
expires it checks whether the bet paid.

Run with `--max-per-host 4` so the second growth is allowed to happen:

```text
round 0: p50= 362.7 ms -> grow -> reproduced-1 registered=True
round 3: p50= 136.7 ms -> grow -> reproduced-2 registered=True
round 4: p50= 863.2 ms members=4
round 5: retire: rollback: 479 ms after growing reproduced-2 against 104 ms
         before it; authorised by the reproduce grant; blocked=['reproduced-2']
round 6: p50= 124.3 ms members=3
round 7: p50= 148.5 ms members=3
```

The loop undid its own change and recovered inside two rounds. Round 114's
equivalent run ended at 791.8 ms with no way back.

**Authorisation.** The rollback does not invent an approval: `Retirer`
retires under a *named* grant, and a rollback passes `reproduce` - the same
grant that authorised the growth authorises undoing it. The journal records
the reversal, the root's blocklist makes it stick, and the grant audit names
the authority. A rollback also fails closed if that grant has expired.

## Why the asymmetry is now deliberate

Round 113 noted that growth needs no operator and retirement does. That is
still true and now has a precise shape:

```text
grow            -> needs the reproduce grant (operator approved once)
undo my growth  -> covered by that same grant
remove a peer   -> still needs a fresh retire grant
```

A swarm can expand, correct its own expansion, and still cannot unilaterally
shrink below the members an operator gave it.

## Verification

19 smoke tests, extended with the host-awareness rule: `co_located` counts
only same-host *and* same-lineage members, a different host or a different
lineage does not count, and the supervisor holds when the candidate is
co-located. `retirement_check` (a peer is retired, under the `retire` grant),
`elastic_loop_check` (sleep-based members, where growth helps), `swarm_check`
and `autonomy_check` all pass.

## Open

- host identity is the hostname in a stem's advertised URL. Two containers on
  one machine with different addresses look like two machines, and one
  machine behind two addresses looks like two machines - the signal is a
  good first approximation, not a fact.
- the bet is evaluated once, after one cooldown. A growth that helps slowly
  and hurts quickly would be rolled back prematurely; a growth that helps
  briefly and hurts later would not be.
- rollback retires the node the growth produced. If that node has since
  started doing useful work of its own - it is a member like any other - the
  reversal removes it anyway. Capacity accounting does not yet exist.
