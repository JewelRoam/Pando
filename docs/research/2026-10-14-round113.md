# Research Round 113 — Closing the elasticity loop

Date: 2026-10-14

## What was open

Reproduction can add a device (rounds 105-108), the adaptive quorum decides
how much checking to spend per decision (rounds 109-110), and retirement can
remove a device (round 111). Nothing decided *when* to do any of it. The
swarm could change size; it could not choose to.

This round also closes a smaller hole round 112 left: a declared category
with no matching `[gating.<category>]` table - a typo - silently produced a
category that contributed no floor of its own.

## Category validation

```text
question 'a': category 'categoy' has no [gating.categoy] table
              (known: ['security', 'ordinary'])
```

`ordinary` is always valid (it is the top-level `[gating]` table); anything
else must name a table that exists. A playbook now fails at load rather than
at decision time.

## The supervisor

`pandosi/elastic.py: ElasticSupervisor` reads the root's own signals from a
new `GET /status` (members, quorum_k, floor and ceiling, whether the quorum
is adaptive, the latency target, and the decision EWMA) and answers one
question: **grow, retire, or leave it alone?**

The rule that matters is what it refuses. It grows only when decisions are
slow, the quorum is already at its floor, and there are members to spare -
because:

```text
slow + quorum head-room  -> a policy question: spend checking, not hardware
slow at the member ceiling -> a per-decision-cost problem, not capacity
slow at the floor with room to grow -> a capacity problem: grow
```

Retirement is the mirror: a member above the latency ceiling while the swarm
is above its `keep` size.

## Measured loop

`tools/elastic_loop_check.py`: a root with the adaptive quorum pinned by a
ceiling of 2 and a floor of 1, two serialized replicas at 30 ms, six
concurrent decisions, target 50 ms. The supervisor reads `/status`, decides,
and calls a grow callback that launches another replica (a stand-in for the
reproduction path verified end to end in round 108).

```text
round 0: p50=  92.8 ms members=2 k=1 -> grow
round 1: p50=  62.4 ms members=3 k=1 -> hold
round 2: p50=  60.6 ms members=3 k=1 -> hold
round 3: p50=  61.5 ms members=3 k=1 -> hold
round 4: p50=  61.5 ms members=3 k=1 -> grow
```

The swarm noticed it was too slow with no checking left to spend, added a
member, and latency fell from 92.8 ms to about 61 ms. It then held for three
rounds - the hysteresis is visible, which is the point - before deciding
again.

## Two honest findings from the run

**The root's view of latency is not the client's.** At rounds 1-3 the client
measured 60-62 ms while the supervisor held, because the supervisor compares
the *root's* decision EWMA - which measures fan-out and vote collection -
against `target x 1.1`. Client-side HTTP and connection overhead are not in
it. The first run of this check failed for exactly that reason (91 ms
observed, 87.5 ms threshold) and it looked like the policy was broken when
it was the signal that was narrower than the question.

**Hysteresis is doing real work.** `pressure_factor` started at 1.25 and the
supervisor never fired; at 1.1 it fires and still does not oscillate. A
pressure factor below 1.0 would make the swarm grow on noise.

## Changes

- `playbook`: a declared category must resolve to a `[gating.<category>]`
  table, or `ordinary`.
- `rootd`: `GET /status` exposing the signals a supervisor needs.
- `pandosi/elastic.py`: `Supervision` and `ElasticSupervisor` (`decide` is a
  pure function of an injectable status; `step` executes through callbacks
  and journals every decision, including the refusals).
- `tools/elastic_loop_check.py`: the closed loop, with its trajectory
  recorded per run under `metrics/elastic-checks/`.

## Verification

19 smoke tests, including a new one pinning the policy's four refusals
(quorum head-room, member ceiling, within target, no signal) and that a
static quorum does not get the floor exemption. `swarm_check`,
`retirement_check` and `autonomy_check` all pass with `/status` and the new
policy in place.

## Open

- growth in the check launches a local replica; wiring the supervisor to
  `reproduce()` - with its playbook, payload, attestation and grant chain -
  is the next step, and it is a smaller step than it sounds because both
  halves are already verified separately.
- the supervisor has no memory: it re-decides from scratch each round, with
  no cooldown after a growth and no notion of how long a change takes to have
  an effect. The hysteresis is in the threshold, not in the controller.
- retirement here still needs the operator's `retire` grant, so an unattended
  swarm can only ever grow. That is the safe default and the asymmetry is
  worth stating: this system can expand without a human and needs one to
  contract.
