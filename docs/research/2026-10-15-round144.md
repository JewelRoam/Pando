# Research Round 144 — A portable combiner, and the smallest real fleet

Date: 2026-10-15

## The constraint round 143 exposed

Round 142's artifact (spec 2) carried per-lineage feature cells: which *named*
member dissented, and what each believed. That tied the file to the quorum it
was fitted on - a fleet with different members would feed the model zeros -
and it was the last thing keeping the combiner from being used by any swarm
that speaks the same question schema.

`pandosi/combiner.py` is now **spec 3**: the per-lineage block is gone, and
the features are per-candidate support (counts and confidence mass), the size
of the majority and the number of distinct choices, plus the appended text
posterior - **27 features**, membership-agnostic.

## Removing features made it better

| on split C (240 rows, unseen) | full coverage | the 77 declined rows |
|---|---:|---:|
| shipped gate | 77.08% | - |
| best single member | 73.75% | 40.26% |
| artifact, spec 2 (per-lineage) | 82.92% | 62.34% |
| **artifact, spec 3 (agnostic)** | **85.00%** | **67.53%** |
| oracle (answer in the trio) | 85.83% | 71.43% |

The agnostic model is **0.83 points from the trio's oracle** at full coverage
and captures **94% of the available headroom** on the rows the gate declines
(67.53% of an oracle 71.43%). The per-lineage cells were encoding "which named
member dissented", which does not generalise; dropping them raised held-out
accuracy and removed the deployment constraint in the same edit.

The advisory property survives: 0 of 77 suggestions land on an `auto` row.

## The smallest real fleet, measured end to end

Everything before this round was measured by replaying recorded votes. With a
membership-agnostic artifact, a live fleet no longer has to match the trio it
was fitted on, so `tools/live_fleet_quality_check.py` runs the whole split
through a real root over HTTP with two stems:

| stem | what it is |
|---|---|
| `accel` | the Laya `english` lineage on the Intel iGPU (OpenVINO) |
| `small` | the **19 KB text member** shipped in `assets/` |

240 held-out rows, `quorum_k=2`, in **13 seconds**:

| outcome | rows | accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|
| auto-executed | 76 | **97.37%** |
| declined, with a combiner suggestion | 164 | 68.29% |
| **a decided answer** | **186 / 240** | **77.50%** |
| oracle (answer in the two members) | - | 86.67% |

Three things are worth reading off that table.

**What the fleet auto-executes is almost always right** - 97.4% of 76 rows.
The gate's caution is doing exactly what it was built for, on live members
rather than replayed ones.

**The advisory combiner turns two thirds of the declines into answers**, 68.3%
of 164 rows, which is where the decided-answer figure comes from. On this
fleet it is *better* on the declined rows than the offline trio's combiner was
(68.29% against 67.53%) - the smaller fleet declines more and thereby routes
more rows into the advised path.

**The whole thing costs one accelerator and 19 KB.** No weights to fetch for
the small stem, no privileges, no GPU for the combiner.

## Where this leaves the project

The objective asks for an elastically scalable, heterogeneous, reproducible
unified intelligence. Rounds 118-144 have now measured each word:

- **heterogeneous**: three execution units on one device, answering
  identically on 240/240 rows, scheduled by what each costs its host;
- **elastic**: k divides throughput, correlated members cost nothing once
  counted as one opinion, and a fleet can grow for capacity without corrupting
  a decision;
- **reproducible**: a member is born in ~29 s and adds nothing unless it
  brings a new opinion;
- **intelligence**: the swarm beats its best member by 3.3 points on its own
  and by 9.2 with the advisory combiner, and the smallest real fleet
  auto-executes at 97.4% while answering 77.5% of everything.
