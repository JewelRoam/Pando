# Research Round 159 — Reliability-aware gating: the tiered fleet beats its core (offline)

Date: 2026-10-16

Round 158 falsified the calibration hypothesis and named the mechanism: a
weaker distinct member dilutes a strong one the same way a replica does.
This round tested the fix that mechanism implies — **auto-execution must be
backed by the member whose accuracy is trusted most** — and it works.

## The prompt scaffold was costing the tier 8 points

Task 004, run on the Mac against split D (240 rows):

| mac-llm prompt variant | accuracy | avg latency | parsed probability |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| A: JSON + probability (round 158 baseline) | 0.7375 | 442 ms | 0.9586 (ECE 0.221) |
| **B: candidate-only** | **0.8167** | **139 ms** | none |
| C: `choice|probability` one-liner | 0.7500 | 247 ms | 0.9130 |

The JSON+confidence scaffold is not neutral: dropping it gains 7.9 points
and 3.2x latency. A tier's measured strength is partly a prompting artifact.

Assent rule: with ft-v3 in the quorum, a tier=auto row is demoted to verify
unless ft-v3's choice equals the aggregate's; demoted rows go to the shipped
combiner like any other declined row.

## What this establishes

**The first configuration that beats the core.** B+assent decides 236/240
(0.9833) against the pair's 234/240 (0.9750) — while auto-executing 40 more
rows (199 vs 159), every one of them correct. Weak-tier coverage and
strong-tier precision are no longer a trade; the assent rule collects the
former without paying the latter.

**The design rule, stated generally:** in a tiered fleet, a decision may
auto-execute only when the *highest-accuracy member present* is in the
winning coalition. The gate's job is not to average tiers; it is to route
disagreement to the advisor and let the trusted member hold the veto.

**Honest edges.** All of this is offline on recorded votes; D has now seen
several configurations (the +0.83-point margin is two rows, within noise -
the *precision at coverage* improvement is the robust part). B returns no
confidence, assigned a constant p=0.5 here; a deployment needs a confidence
policy for choice-only tiers. And the rule is a five-line filter in the
evaluator, not yet in rootd — Task 005 (dispatched to the Mac) implements it
as an opt-in `--assent-checkpoint`, with tests and a branch to review.
