# Research Round 129 — Count the opinions, not the devices

Date: 2026-10-15

## What round 128 left to build

Round 128 measured the cliff and the mechanism: four cheap members, each
decorrelated from the neural trio but correlated with each other (phi up to
+0.898), override a unanimous trio on 37 rows and are wrong on 32 of them,
leaving the swarm 11.6 points worse than the trio alone. Reweighting the
choice did not help, because the problem is not how the votes are weighed:
a near-copy gets a vote of its own.

Round 121 had already answered that for replicas, by collapsing votes that
share a checkpoint name. The generalisation is to collapse the *group*.

## The change

- `pandosi/common.py`: `collapse_lineages(votes, blocs)` groups by bloc id
  when one is given, and `aggregate(..., blocs=...)` collapses before
  counting, exactly as `count_lineages` does for names.
- `pandosi/playbook.py`: `[lineages] blocs = [["a", "b"], ...]`, validated -
  every member must be admitted, no member twice, a bloc of one is refused
  because that is already the default.
- `pandosi/rootd.py`: `Coordinator.blocs`, and the quorum's floor,
  `corroboration_floor` and the reported counts now measure **opinions**
  rather than stems (`lineages` and `opinions` are both reported).

A playbook with no `blocs` produces an empty map and behaves exactly as
before, which is what the 19-case smoke suite pins.

## The measurement

The same seven members, the same 240 wording-held-out rows, paired bootstrap
over 2000 resamples against the trio at 77.08%:

| scenario | full coverage | auto | vs trio | better in |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| trio alone | 77.08% | 172 | - | - |
| trio + `centroid` only | **80.42%** | 132 | +3.39 | **100.0%** |
| trio + all four, counted by name | 65.42% | 120 | -11.62 | 0.1% |
| trio + all four, one bloc | 77.92% | 158 | +0.84 | 61.9% |

Declaring the family a bloc recovers **12.5 points** (65.42% -> 77.92%) and
returns the swarm to the trio's level, with the family's information kept and
its vote count not. The distortion was never about the members being weak: it
was four votes where there was one opinion.

## What this gives the elastic premise

The project can now say something it could not before: **a fleet may grow with
same-family devices at no cost to the decision.** Add four correlated devices
for throughput and the swarm's accuracy stays where it was (77.92% against
77.08%, ahead in 61.9% of resamples - a wash) instead of collapsing to 65.4%.
Growth for *intelligence* still requires a new opinion; growth for *capacity*
is now free.

That is the shape a self-replicating swarm needs: replication is how the
system gets more compute, and the aggregation rule is what keeps replication
from rewriting its answers.

## The open question this round found

A bloc has to speak with one voice, and the current one - the choice carrying
the most probability mass across its members - is measurably not the best
available. Against delegating to the member with the best probe accuracy, the
consensus loses 2.48 points (ahead in only 7.3% of resamples, though the 95%
interval [-6.25, +1.25] still contains zero at 240 rows).

So "how should a bloc speak?" is round 130's question, with three candidates
already on the table: the mass-weighted consensus (shipped), the most
competent member, and a competence-weighted consensus. The same machinery
that declares blocs can declare which rule they use.
