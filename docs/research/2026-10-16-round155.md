# Research Round 155 — The growth ladder, priced: what each added device buys

Date: 2026-10-16

Rounds 138-146 measured growth and retirement by opinions: a new opinion
helps, a replica does not, removal is free. Round 154 changed the arithmetic
underneath — opinions are counted (`--lineage-quorum`), the advisor sees the
counted set, and the member that arrived by fine-tuning decides 0.9417 alone.
So the ladder got its second measurement: live, on the real accelerators,
over the full 240 rows of split D, with the offline prediction written down
*before* anything was started.

## What each added device actually bought

**A replica is worse than nothing: it costs latency for no information.**
Adding a second english stem left every answer identical (same auto rows,
same accuracy, same decisions) and cut throughput by 56% — 17.25 to 7.59
rows/s. The root waits for k stems and gets one opinion back; the slower
replica sets the pace. "A replica buys nothing" (round 146) now has a price
tag on the other side of the ledger: it *spends*.

**A new opinion buys +23.3 points.** Adding the fine-tuned member to the same
devices: decided 0.7417 -> 0.9750, auto-executed rows 0.8454 -> **1.0000**
accuracy at 159 rows, and the advisor's own accuracy on the declined rows
0.3043 -> 0.9259. The advisor's value is not fixed; it compounds with the
opinion set it is given (round 154's lesson, seen from the growth side).

**Removal is not merely free — it pays.** Retiring the replica left the
answers bit-identical (same opinions) and nearly doubled throughput: 8.16 ->
16.07 rows/s. The three-device fleet's *ceiling* was set by its slowest
useless member; the elastic system now has a measured reason to prefer a
trade: one opinion that disagrees usefully is worth more than any number of
stems that agree redundantly.

## The currency of elastic growth

Put together with rounds 121-154, the swarm's growth policy now has a
measurable exchange rate:

- **opinions in -> intelligence** (0.7417 -> 0.9750 for one new opinion);
- **stems in -> latency** (replica: +1 stem, -56% throughput);
- **stems out -> throughput**, when they were replicas (retire: +97%
  throughput, 0 points of quality);
- and every one of those statements is only true while the quorum counts
  opinions and the advisor sees the counted set.

## Honest notes

- Stage 1-2's advisor (0.3043 on 46 declined rows) is the combiner outside
  its fitting distribution: it was fitted on multi-opinion fleets, and a
  single vote gives it almost nothing to arbitrate. The number is reported
  as measured, not as the advisor's steady-state skill.
- Throughput here is a single sequential client, so it measures *quorum
  latency*, not fleet capacity; the round-133 concurrency ceiling still
  governs how many such clients a fleet can serve.
- The iGPU-NPU float differences did not move any aggregate: stages 1 and 2
  are identical to the digit on every quality metric.
