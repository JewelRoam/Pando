# Research Round 111 — Per-category floors, and the right to remove a device

Date: 2026-10-14

## Two halves of the same trade

Round 110 gave the swarm an adaptive quorum size: under latency pressure it
gives up voters, because in this architecture voters-per-decision is the
currency that buys decisions-in-parallel. That left two things unfinished:
*how much* checking is non-negotiable, and what happens to a member the swarm
should not be paying for at all.

## Per-category quorum floors

The floor is the same knob as "how much agreement am I willing to give up",
and it is not the same answer for every question. The playbook already
distinguishes security decisions for its gating thresholds, so the floor now
comes from the same place:

```toml
[gating]
quorum_floor = 2

[gating.security]
quorum_floor = 4
```

Six serialized replicas, adaptive quorum, target 120 ms, CLI floor 1:

| question | concurrency | throughput | p50 | quorum settled |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ordinary ("approve?") | 1 | 12.6 req/s | 78.9 ms | 5 |
| ordinary | 8 | 33.3 req/s | 251.5 ms | **2** |
| security ("security threat?") | 1 | 12.6 req/s | 77.9 ms | 5 |
| security | 8 | 25.9 req/s | 256.2 ms | **4** |

Under load the ordinary decision gave up voters down to its floor of 2; the
security decision stopped at 4 and kept **twice the independent voters for
22% less throughput**. Both kept the full quorum of 5 while idle.

The floors are *binding*, and that is worth stating plainly: at concurrency 8
neither question reached the 120 ms target (251 / 256 ms), because the floor
forbade the further reduction that would have been needed. The policy is
therefore "spend checking down to a floor trying to reach the target", not
"reach the target". A target that cannot be met inside the floors is telling
the operator to grow the swarm, not to weaken it.

Round 99 is the reason this matters: disagreement is where the errors are
(16.7% accuracy on splits), so the floor is where the swarm refuses to buy
throughput with accuracy.

## Retirement: the half of elasticity that removes a device

Reproduction grows a swarm; nothing removed a member. A slow, idle, expensive
device stayed a voter forever - and because a stem re-registers every five
seconds, deleting it from the registry would last five seconds. So retirement
is a decision *plus* a durable effect: the root keeps a blocklist and refuses
the retired node's re-registration.

`tools/retirement_check.py`, one deliberately slow member among three fast
ones:

```text
members before: {member-1: 45.6, member-2: 37.4, member-0: 38.1, member-3: 718.3}
1. no grant   -> acted=[False] reason=retire refused: missing (no retire grant)
2. granted    -> retired member-3: slowest voter above 150 ms while 4 members
                 are live; blocked=['member-3']
3. durable    -> members=['member-0','member-1','member-2']   (survived heartbeats)
4. still serving -> choice=approve responded=3
```

The selection signal is clean (718 ms against 38-46 ms), removal goes through
the same grant ledger and journal as reproduction, and the swarm keeps
answering on what remains. The policy is deliberately not "retire the
slowest": it refuses when the swarm is at its `keep` size, and it refuses to
retire a member that has never been exercised - a device that is slow but is
the only one answering is the swarm, not a problem.

## Changes

- `Coordinator._category_floor` + `_adapt_quorum(question)`: the adaptive
  floor is per question category, from the playbook's `gating`.
- `Coordinator.retire` + a `blocked` map; `register()` now returns whether the
  node was accepted; `rootd` adds `POST /retire` and answers `403` to a
  retired node's heartbeat.
- `pandosi/elastic.py`: `choose_retirement` (pure policy) and `Retirer`
  (grant-checked, journaled).
- `grants`: a `retire` action.
- `tools/scaling_law_check.py`: `--question` and `--playbook`, so category
  floors are measurable in a sweep.

## Verification

18 smoke tests (a new one pins the per-category floors, including the
binding-floor behaviour and the no-playbook fallback); `swarm_check`,
`slow_stem_check`, `autonomy_check` and `service_check` all pass with the
retirement route and the `register()` return change in place.

## Open

- category detection is a keyword match on the question text ("security",
  "suspicious", "phishing", "threat"). It is the weak link in the whole
  per-category scheme: a playbook question named `risk_check` would silently
  get the ordinary floor.
- retirement is one node per sweep and manual; nothing watches for a member
  that has become slow *and* useless over time, which is what would make this
  a closed loop alongside reproduction.
- a retired member is blocked forever. There is no way to re-admit a device
  whose problem was fixed, and no expiry on the blocklist.
