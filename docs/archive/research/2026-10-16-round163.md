# Research Round 163 — The 16GB tier budget: coexistence costs the tail, not the mean

Date: 2026-10-16

The user's question underneath the whole project - "under a given hardware
budget, which intelligence is optimal?" - got its first controlled
measurement: keep the Mac's Qwen3-8B serving 240 fixed rows, with and
without the 4B tier resident beside it (Task 012; both tiers online again
afterwards).

| condition | 8B tok/s (mean) | p50 | p95 | 8B RSS | 4B RSS | swap used |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A: 4B + 8B resident | 25.42 | 248.3 ms | **301.0 ms** | 1947 MB | 5616 MB | 20.6 GB |
| B: 8B alone | 25.52 | 245.8 ms | **250.7 ms** | 1975 MB | - | 20.5 GB |

## What the numbers say

**Coexistence is free in the mean and expensive in the tail.** Mean
throughput moves -0.37% and p50 by 1%; p95 rises about 20% when a second
tier lives on the same 16 GB. The second tier's footprint (5.6 GB RSS; the
8B's own stays put) is the visible cost, and memory contention shows up in
the slow tail, not the average.

**The tail is where our quorums live.** Every fleet in this project asks
all members per decision and waits for the slowest, so a 20% tail penalty
on the LLM tier propagates straight into row latency; the round-162 live
four-tier run (p50 435 ms, 2.25 rows/s) is that effect, priced.

**The practical budget rule for 16 GB:** prefer one resident LLM tier per
machine role - the 8B when coverage matters (it automates the most rows,
all correct), the 4B when the machine must also do other work - and treat
co-residency as a deliberate tail-latency purchase, not a default.

## Honest edges

- RSS under mmap under-reports weights (weights are file-backed; the 8B's
  ~2 GB RSS is mostly KV and runtime), so the 5.6 GB figure is the 4B's
  resident share, not its full disk footprint.
- Swap sat near 20.5 GB in both conditions - dominated by the machine's
  other processes, so this run cannot attribute swap to our servers; the
  p95 delta is the trustworthy signal.
- One machine, one model pair, one suite (E, 240 rows).
