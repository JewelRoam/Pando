# Research Round 131 — The NPU is a graph problem, not a hardware problem

Date: 2026-10-15

## The blocker round 130 left

Round 130 put the CPU and the Intel iGPU in one fleet and left the NPU out
with a precise diagnosis: the OpenVINO vpux compiler rejected the graph at
`node_MatMul_215` with non-broadcastable dimensions (`-1` against
`INT64_MIN`), after the front end failed to infer upper bounds for
`gnr1_gather_el` (GatherElements) and `node_bitwise_and_1` (LogicalAnd).

The name `gnr1_gather_el` was the clue. That node is not in the original
checkpoint - it is the output of this repository's own `tools/fix_gathernd.py`,
which replaced a GatherND with Reshape/Slice/Gather/GatherElements so the
Intel GPU plugin would accept it (round 8). The GPU fix had become the NPU's
blocker.

## The graph that compiles

Four artifacts, compiled on the NPU as they stand:

| artifact | nodes | IsNaN | GatherND | GatherElements | NPU |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `laya-en-static.onnx` | 1619 | 30 | 1 | 1 | rejected |
| `laya-en-static-fixed.onnx` | 1629 | 30 | 0 | 2 | rejected |
| **`laya-en-static-nonisnan.onnx`** | **1599** | 30 | 0 | 2 | **compiles, 39.2s** |
| `laya-en-static128-fixed.onnx` | 1621 | 30 | 0 | 2 | rejected |

The one that compiles is the variant that carries *both* earlier repairs -
the GatherND rewrite and whatever the "nonisnan" pass removed, 20 nodes fewer
than the fixed graph. Compilation takes 39 seconds, then the backend lazily
compiles the concrete input shape (sequence length 256, marker width 8) and
votes.

Nothing about the silicon had to change. The NPU was always able to run this
model; the graph it was handed carried a repair for a different accelerator's
op gaps.

## Verification against the CPU

The same weights, three execution units, 240 wording-held-out tasks each,
asked directly over loopback:

| unit | runtime | accuracy | median latency | throughput |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| CPU | ONNX Runtime | 75.42% | 94 ms | 10.0 dec/s |
| Intel iGPU | OpenVINO | 75.42% | **53 ms** | **17.6 dec/s** |
| Intel NPU | OpenVINO | 75.42% | 112 ms | 8.7 dec/s |

**All three agree on all 240 rows** - the iGPU with a median confidence gap
of 0.0002 against the CPU, the NPU with 0.0003. Three execution units, two
runtimes, one voter.

Registered as five stems - the three units running `english`, plus
`typed-decisions` and the cheap text member - the fleet reports **3 opinions
for 5 stems** and reaches 77.92% majority accuracy, the same value the
CPU-only fleet reached in round 130. The extra silicon bought capacity, not
information; the lineage rule is what makes that true by construction rather
than by luck.

One honest note on the NPU's value here: at 8.7 decisions per second it is
**slower than the CPU** (10.0) and half the iGPU (17.6) on this model. Its
case is efficiency per watt, which this measurement cannot see - it is not a
throughput win, and nothing in these numbers should be read as one.

## What this says about heterogeneous execution

Each accelerator in this project has needed its own graph dialect:

| unit | what it rejects | what fixed it |
|---|---|---|
| Intel GPU (OpenVINO) | GatherND (no layout) | Reshape/Slice/Gather/GatherElements |
| Intel NPU (vpux) | the GPU's repair, plus a shape it cannot bound | the `nonisnan` pass |
| CPU (ONNX Runtime) | nothing | - |

A fleet that spans units therefore cannot share one artifact unless the
artifacts are generated and verified per unit - which is what the project's
spore transfer already carries (a lineage record plus an artifact digest),
and what an "admit this device" decision has to include. The measurement
above is the second time a fix for one unit became the blocker for another;
it will not be the last, and it is a reason to treat graph variants as
lineage-level artifacts rather than as local build details.
