## Reading it honestly

**Zero-shot 4B is not smarter than the tuned small model on this task family.**
Qwen3-4B lands at 0.7375 - within a point of untuned Laya english (0.7292) -
while the 3070's fine-tuned Laya decides 0.9417. "Bigger model" alone buys
nothing here; the self-trained member is still the best single tier.

**The LLM tier's confidence is a poison and a lever at once.** Its mean
reported probability is 0.9586 against 0.7375 accuracy (ECE 0.2211, every
vote in the top bin). Seating it swings the gate wide open: auto coverage
159 -> 225 rows. But the system's decided accuracy *falls* 0.9750 -> 0.9167:
the gate is being bought by a foreign, uncalibrated confidence scale. The
combination layer - not the model - is now the bottleneck, exactly the
falsification branch round 157 named in advance.

**A third complaint is a real signal.** With mac-llm seated, 225 rows
auto-execute at 0.9244. If calibration keeps the coverage and recovers the
accuracy, the three-tier fleet beats the pair; if it cannot, tiered
composition needs a confidence-policy per tier, not just per member.

## Update — the prediction, tested and falsified

Task 003 delivered mac-llm votes on splits C (0.6542 accuracy, mean p 0.9574)
and B (0.8500, 0.9622). A temperature fitted on C - T=4.09, ECE 0.3033 ->
0.0336, and a B-fitted T=1.79 as cross-check - does not change the split-D
outcomes:

| configuration | full | auto | auto acc | decided |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| english + ft-v3 (pair) | 0.9417 | 159 | 1.0000 | **0.9750** |
| trio, mac-llm raw | 0.9000 | 225 | 0.9244 | 0.9167 |
| trio, mac-llm T_C | 0.9250 | 224 | 0.9241 | 0.9167 |
| trio, mac-llm T_B | 0.9125 | 225 | 0.9244 | 0.9167 |

**The prediction (auto >= 200, decided >= 0.9750) is falsified: coverage
held at ~224 but decided accuracy stayed at 0.9167.** Calibration is real -
it fixed the confidence scale - but it was never the binding constraint.

**The mechanism, named:** the trio auto-executes whenever any two of three
agree. With english and mac-llm at ~0.73 solo, their pairwise agreements
become auto rows at ~0.924, while the pair's disagreements escalated to an
advisor that scored 0.926 on exactly those rows - the gate's selectivity,
not its arithmetic, was the quality. So a **distinct weaker member dilutes a
strong one the same way a replica does** (round 154's disease, new cause:
accuracy rather than correlation or confidence). Composition lifts a system
only when the added tier is *at least as strong* as what it outvotes.

**Two follow-ups exist and are dispatched/queued.** Task 004 (dispatched,
running on the Mac): three prompt formats on split D - the round-003 control
raised C from 0.6542 to 0.7958 by dropping the JSON+confidence scaffold, so
the tier's accuracy is partly a prompting artifact, not a model ceiling. And
a queued design question: reliability-aware gating (auto-execution requires
the measured-strongest member's assent) - to be measured before it is
believed.
