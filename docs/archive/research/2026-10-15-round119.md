# Research Round 119 — A trained checkpoint that decides exactly like its parent

Date: 2026-10-15

## The experiment round 118 asked for

Round 118 closed with a plan: the three admitted lineages are prompt variants
whose errors arrive together (phi up to +0.50), only 5.6% of tasks separate
the weighted vote from the best member, and at that discordance resolving a
one-point difference would take ~3,900 tasks. The next compute should
therefore buy *diversity*, not volume.

The repository's one candidate for that is `artifacts/laya-rlcd-v7.onnx` -
the v7 candidate from the RTX 3070 RLCD run (rounds 79-81), a checkpoint
whose weights were changed by training rather than by re-rendering a prompt.
`tools/ensemble_value_check.py` grew a `--lineages` flag for the test, and
this round ran the trio `english, typed-decisions, rlcd-v7` over the same
240-task dev split the round-118 trio ran on.

## Result: not one decision in 240 moved

| pair | same choice | disagreement |
|---|---:|---:|
| `english` x `rlcd-v7` | **240/240** | **0.0%** |
| `english` x `typed-decisions` | 206/240 | 12.1% |
| `rlcd-v7` x `typed-decisions` | 206/240 | 12.1% |

The two graphs are not the same file - `laya-en.onnx.data` and
`laya-rlcd-v7.onnx.data` have different SHA-256 digests, and the checkpoint
has its own calibration table (T=2.6704 against english's 3.2). Their
*decisions* are identical on every task, and their calibrated logits track
each other with a near-constant scale (ratio 0.73 +- 0.07 on the 176 rows
with a decisive confidence). What the RLCD round changed, on this suite, was
the confidence scale, not the decision boundary.

For scale: two independently trained models of this size disagree on
10-25% of a suite like this one (the other two pairs here do exactly that).
Zero disagreements in 240 is not a small effect, it is a different
hypothesis - the descendant reproduces its parent's decisions.

The error correlation says the same thing more sharply: `english` x
`rlcd-v7` has **phi = +1.000**, disagreement 0.000, and both-wrong 6.86x
the independence rate.

## What that does to the quorum

| source | at gate coverage (193) | full coverage |
|---|---:|---:|
| `member:typed-decisions` | **97.4%** | **90.0%** |
| `min_conf` (veto) | 96.4% | 88.7% |
| `margin` / `agreement` | 96.9% | 85.4% |
| `max_conf` | 94.3% | 86.3% |
| `gate` (shipped) | 93.3% | 85.4% |
| `member:english` | 94.3% | 85.4% |
| `member:rlcd-v7` | 94.3% | 85.4% |

Oracle 93.75% (down from 96.25% for the round-118 trio).

The swarm got **worse by admitting a member**. On the round-118 trio the
weighted vote reached 90.4% at full coverage; here it reaches 85.4%. And
85.4% is not a coincidence: it is exactly `english`'s own accuracy, and
exactly `rlcd-v7`'s own accuracy, to four decimals. That is a theorem, not a
statistic. When two of three members always agree, the majority is that
pair's answer, so the majority's accuracy *is* the duplicated member's
accuracy - the third member's votes can only ever break a tie that cannot
occur.

So a quorum containing a duplicate is a single model with extra steps. The
paired bootstrap agrees from the other direction: the best rule available
in this trio (`min_conf`) loses to simply trusting `typed-decisions` by
1.26 points at full coverage, and does so in 95.1% of resamples.

`min_conf` being the best rule here is worth one line, because round 117
called it a curiosity and round 118 called it a coin flip. In a trio with a
duplicated pair it is the *only* rule that can express "the pair disagreed in
confidence" - with phi = +1.000, the least-confident member is the one
carrying any information at all. It is a symptom of the redundancy, not a
better combiner.

## Why this matters more than the number

The project's central claim is elastic self-replication: copy the lineage
onto more devices and the swarm grows. This round is the first direct
measurement of what replication does to the *decision*, and the answer is
that it is worse than neutral:

- A replica is a correlated voter with phi near 1.0, so a quorum of
  replicas has the accuracy of one replica and the vote count of many.
- Admitting one duplicate cost 5.0 accuracy points, more than any combiner
  in the sweep can win back (the best combiner's edge over the best member
  is 1.0 point).
- The self-replication path the project already runs (rounds 107-116)
  reproduces stems for *capacity*; nothing in it distinguishes a reproduction
  from a new lineage, and the admission list cannot tell them apart either.

The actionable form is not "stop replicating". It is that a quorum has to be
counted over **lineages, not stems**. `Coordinator.pick_quorum` already does
the right thing where it can: it round-robins across checkpoint groups, so a
k-voter quorum takes one voter per distinct checkpoint before it takes a
second from any group (rounds 109-110 are why it also orders by load). But
when k exceeds the number of distinct checkpoints, which is precisely the
state an elastically replicated swarm converges to, a duplicate is admitted
with **full weight and no discount**, and the vote silently becomes the
duplicated lineage's own decision. Round 119 is the measurement of what that
costs, and it costs more than any combiner in the project can win back.

## What this does not say

- It does not say RLCD training cannot produce diversity; it says *this*
  export did not. The candidate was fitted on 22 labeled items and its own
  calibration write-up notes the holdout showed no improvement. A fine-tune
  that never moved a decision on held-out data was never going to move one
  here.
- It does not re-open the round-118 result. That trio's members are three
  genuinely different graphs and their edge over the best member is
  unaffected.
- It does not measure the quantized graph (`laya-en.int8.onnx`), which is a
  second same-lineage candidate for the same test.

## Next

Real diversity has to come from somewhere the project has not tried:
a different base model, a different tokenizer (which is what makes
`multilingual` the one genuinely decorrelated member), a different
architecture, or an actual fine-tune on data the base cannot already
separate. The cheapest decisive test of the last one is to check whether a
candidate disagrees with its parent *at all* before it is admitted - one
number, computed on any labeled suite, and this round exists because nobody
had computed it.
