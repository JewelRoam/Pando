# Research Round 156 — The latency target buys the fast twin, and k stops buying replicas

Date: 2026-10-16

Round 155 priced growth: opinions buy intelligence, stems spend latency. This
round made the adaptive machinery spend by that price. Live fleet: the iGPU
(english), the NPU (english), the RTX 3070 (ft-v3), one sequential client,
split D's 240 rows, adaptive quorum, latency target 100 ms, floor 2. The
prediction, written first: decided 0.9750 in every configuration that keeps
the two opinions; the *seats* the target buys decide the latency.

| live run over 240 rows | seats | k | p50 | rows/s | decided |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| before round 156 | NPU seated (230 rows), cost-ranked | 2 | 115 ms | 8.04 | 0.9750 |
| + latency-ranked seats | iGPU seated (240) | **oscillates 2<->3** | 77 ms | 11.54 | 0.9750 |
| + opinion ceiling (final) | iGPU seated (240), NPU asked once | **2 stable** | **60 ms** | **15.64** | 0.9750 |

## Defect 1: the target could not buy the fast twin

`pick_quorum` ranked members inside a lineage by host-cost bucket (round 134)
- and on this machine the cheapest english member is the NPU: ~5-7 ms of host
CPU per decision, ~110 ms per decision. The iGPU costs ~39-56 ms of host CPU
and serves in ~40 ms. With a 100 ms target the root shrank to k=2 and seated
the NPU on 230 of 240 rows, settling at **p50 115 ms - fifteen percent over
its own stated target - with the 40 ms twin idle**. The cost ranking answered
a question nobody had asked while the question the operator *had* asked went
unanswered.

Fix: the seat ranking is now pressure-aware. With a latency target
configured, rank within a lineage by (least loaded, latency band, cost); with
no target, keep (cost, least loaded, latency band) - round 134's behavior,
unchanged, for fleets that never asked for speed. Live after the fix: the
iGPU took the english seat on 240/240 rows, p50 77 ms.

## Defect 2: k was oscillating, paying 40 ms every other decision for a replica

The fix made the fast pair *too fast*: at k=2 decisions ran 58-60 ms,
comfortably under `target * 0.6` (60 ms), so the adaptive dial asked for one
more voter - and the only voter left was the NPU, a second stem of a lineage
the quorum already counted. k went 2->3 (102 ms) -> shrink -> 2 (58 ms) ->
grow, **fifty-two times in 240 rows**, and every expansion added ~40 ms of
latency and no information. This one was found by running, not by reasoning;
the run's own `[quorum]` chatter is the evidence.

Fix: when the quorum counts opinions, the adaptation ceiling is the number of
*opinions* among selectable members, not the number of stems. A k beyond the
opinion count can only wake a replica. Live after: k settled at 2 and stayed
(one cold-start arrival at 3), no `[quorum]` messages, and the fleet landed
at **p50 60 ms, EWMA 50 ms - under its target - at 15.64 rows/s**, exactly
the configuration round 155's manual ladder had to assemble by hand.

## The measurement that had to change with the semantics

With `--lineage-quorum` the `/decide` response's vote list is the *collapsed
opinion set*: a replica's vote is folded into its lineage, so "who served
this decision" cannot be read from the response at all. Mid-round this made
the NPU look like it answered zero rows while ``failures`` was also zero -
the vote was there, inside `english`. `stemd`'s `/health` now reports the
stem-side truth, a `decisions` counter: the final run shows iGPU 240, 3070
240, NPU 1 (the cold start). Tools that measure fleets under a lineage
quorum should use it; the collapsed list is for the aggregator, not for
accounting.

## What it establishes

- The adaptive dial now spends in opinions: k counts the voices that can
  disagree, not the boxes that can answer.
- A latency target is a purchase order: while it is set, the seat goes to
  the member that can meet it (39 ms host CPU/decision for the iGPU instead
  of 5 for the NPU), and the replica is woken only when its sibling is
  already busy (load leads the ranking) - capacity, not redundancy.
- The elastic loop assembled 155's stage-4 configuration by itself, from a
  worse seat, in three live runs: 0.8292-era plumbing reaching 0.9750 at
  15.64 rows/s with the host CPU 77-80% free.

## Honest notes

- The first decision is still cold: EWMA is unset, so no adaptation happens,
  k=3 is asked, the NPU's first request pays its compile, and that one
  decision costs ~1 s. Unaddressed; it is a startup problem, not a steady
  state one.
- Paying latency with host CPU is a deliberate trade while a target is set.
  With the target removed, the old cost ranking returns and so does the
  cheap slow twin.
- Both fixes are covered by smoke tests (21 total): the fast-twin seat with
  and without a target, and the opinion ceiling under collapse.
