# Research Round 120 — Narrowing the ballot does not resolve the disagreement

Date: 2026-10-15

## The last cheap idea for the 17-point gap

Round 118 located the swarm's headroom precisely: on the 78 disagreements in
the dev split (157 across both splits), the correct answer is present
somewhere in the trio 93.6% of the time, while the best resolution rule over
the same three votes captures 75.6%. Round 118 showed no reweighting of those
votes gets close. The one move left that is not a reweighting is to ask a
*different* question: keep the state, drop the candidates nobody voted for,
and let the trio decide between the options it actually split over.

That is what the playbook's verify tier would do in production, so it is
worth measuring before anything is built on it. `tools/rereask_check.py` runs
it on the 78 disagreements, both ballots scored on the same rows.

## Result: 1.3 points, and the unanimity it creates is not a signal

| rule | first ballot | re-ask | change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| plain majority | 71.8% | 73.1% | +1.3 |
| `margin` (weighted vote) | 75.6% | 76.9% | +1.3 |
| `max_conf` | 66.7% | 71.8% | +5.1 |
| `min_conf` (veto) | 52.6% | 65.4% | +12.8 |
| `member:typed-decisions` | 74.4% | 76.9% | +2.6 |
| `member:english` | 60.3% | 57.7% | -2.6 |
| `member:multilingual` | 30.8% | 59.0% | **+28.2** |

The ballot narrows from 8 candidates to 2.1, the majority's answer changes on
only **12 of 78 rows**, and the best rule gains **1.3 points** of the 18
available.

The routing question is the interesting one, and it fails cleanly. The re-ask
produces a unanimous answer on 31 of the 78 rows - which the first ballot, by
construction, never does. If unanimity after a narrower question meant
anything, the verify tier would have a rule:

| subset after the re-ask | rows | first ballot | re-ask |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| unanimous (settled) | 31 | 83.9% | **83.9%** |
| still split (unsettled) | 47 | 70.2% | 72.3% |

(`margin` on both sides; the plain majority gives the same 83.9% and 63.8% ->
66.0%.) The settled rows were already the easy ones, and after the re-ask they
are answered *identically accurate* - the unanimity is manufactured by the
narrower ballot, not earned by the members. All of the 1.3-point gain sits in
the rows that stayed split.

## Why, and what is closed

The re-ask changes the question. It does not change the members, and round
118 measured what the members are: errors that arrive together at phi up to
+0.50. A narrower ballot cannot decorrelate them.

The one number worth keeping from the `member:multilingual` row: narrowing
the ballot takes it from 30.8% to 59.0%. Its disagreements are mostly "picked
a third option that the other two had already eliminated", not "picked the
wrong one of two". That is a diagnosis of the weakest member, and it is the
kind of thing a per-lineage candidate-restriction policy could act on - but
it lifts a member to 59%, which is still below the vote.

So both routes to the headroom are now closed by measurement:

- **better combination of the same votes**: at most 1.0 point (round 118);
- **a narrower ballot for the same members**: 1.3 points (this round).

The 93.6% that the trio holds on those rows is a *members* problem. Nothing
downstream of the votes reaches it.
