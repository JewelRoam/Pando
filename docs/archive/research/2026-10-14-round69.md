# Research Round 69 — Remote-link degradation and recovery

Date: 2026-10-14

## Experiment

`tools/remote_latency_check.py` puts a local HTTP reverse proxy in front of a
real mock stem. The proxy delay can be changed while root is running, which
simulates a remote link whose latency degrades and later recovers without
changing the stem or quorum code.

Root was configured with:

```text
initial stem timeout: 500 ms
maximum call timeout: 2.0 s
```

Three fast stems and one proxied "remote-like" stem formed the quorum.

## Phases and results

| phase | proxy delay | responded | quorum | fan-out |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| healthy link | 100 ms | 4 | 4 | 188.1 ms |
| degraded link | 1500 ms | 3 | 4 | 1009.3 ms |
| cooldown skip | 1500 ms | 3 | 3 | **16.1 ms** |
| recovered link | 50 ms | 4 | 4 | 56.1 ms |

The remote worker's EWMA settled at 146.3 ms. Its learned timeout was
roughly one second, so the 1.5-second degraded response was rejected while
the remaining three workers answered.

The next immediate decision removed the unhealthy worker from selection and
returned in 16.1 ms. After cooldown, with delay reduced to 50 ms, the worker
was probed again and rejoined the four-worker quorum.

## Interpretation

This validates the intended elastic behavior at the HTTP layer:

- a degrading remote worker is isolated without stopping the swarm;
- cooldown decisions avoid paying the bad link's timeout repeatedly;
- recovery is automatic, so isolation is not permanent blacklisting.

## Limitations

This is controlled latency injection on one physical host, not a real
two-host network. It does not measure packet loss, bandwidth saturation,
NAT/firewall behavior, or real WAN jitter. Those still require a second
physical device.
