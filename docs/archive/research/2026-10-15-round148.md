# Research Round 148 — Calibration does not transfer, and the gate is tuned to it

Date: 2026-10-15

## A backlog item that had quietly unblocked

PROJECT-STATE has carried "rootd calibration from a held-out set — blocked,
needs labelled eval data" since round 100, and the tables the swarm runs on
were fitted on 12-22 items. This session produced 720 labelled rows across
three wording splits, so the blocker is gone and the item could be done.

It did not need any model run either: a recorded report stores calibrated
confidences and the transform is `sigmoid(logit(p) / T)`, which inverts
exactly, so the raw probabilities come back out of the old tables and a new
temperature can be fitted on 240 rows.

## The refit is better where it was fitted and not where it is used

| lineage | shipped T (n=12-22) | refit T (n=240) | dev log-loss |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `english` | 3.2 | **1.5** | 0.326 -> 0.262 |
| `multilingual` | 3.8 | **3.5** | 0.423 -> 0.422 |
| `typed-decisions` | **5.0** | **0.5** | 0.573 -> **0.252** |

On the training split the refit is clearly better - `typed-decisions` had been
softened by a factor of ten against what its own 240 rows want. On the third
wording split, which neither table was fitted on:

| calibration | held-out log-loss |
|---|---:|
| shipped tables | **0.5863** |
| refit on 240 dev rows | 0.5895 |
| fitted on the deployment's own wording | **0.5318** |

So more rows do not help if they come from a different wording distribution;
the fit simply learns that distribution's confidence scale. Fitting on the
deployment's own rows is worth 9% of log-loss - and requires labels from the
deployment, which is the part a shipped table cannot substitute for.

## The finding that matters more than the refit

The downstream effect on the gate is a **regression**:

| | full coverage | auto-executed | accuracy where auto |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| shipped tables | 0.7708 | 163 | 0.9080 |
| refit | 0.7667 | **179** | 0.8603 |

Better-calibrated confidences are *sharper*, so `mean_probability >= 0.6` is
satisfied more often and the gate auto-executes 16 more rows at four points
lower accuracy. **The shipped thresholds are tuned to the shipped tables'
conservatism**, and the old temperatures' error - a five-fold over-softening
of `typed-decisions` - was quietly acting as a safety margin.

Calibration and gating are therefore one system, not two: a temperature change
is a threshold change, and any future recalibration has to re-examine
`auto_min_probability` in the same edit. The project's own calibration policy
("a vote on an unknown confidence scale cannot justify an autonomous action",
round 100) is right; what this round adds is that the *known* scale is
distribution-specific.

## What was updated because of this

PROJECT-STATE's "not yet done" list was stale in two places and both are now
corrected: the NPU op-analysis item was solved in round 131, and this
calibration item is not blocked by data - it is answered, negatively, by the
