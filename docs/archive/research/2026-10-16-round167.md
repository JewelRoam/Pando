# Research Round 167 — The reasoning axis: thinking buys nothing here

Date: 2026-10-16

Round 162 showed a stronger tier buys coverage; round 164 showed the
witness is per family. The next candidate axis was cheaper than a bigger
model: the *same* Qwen3-8B with `enable_thinking=true` - reasoning tokens
before the answer. Task 018 ran it (B prompt, temperature 0, max_tokens
512) against the standard mode on the same splits:

| split | thinking acc | standard acc | delta | avg latency | est. reasoning share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| E (240 rows) | 0.9250 | 0.9250 | 0.0000 | 9.44 s | 98.2% |
| F (120 rows) | 0.9750 | 0.9833 | -0.0083 | 7.80 s | 96.4% |

**Verdict: no.** Thinking tied standard on E, lost a point on F, and paid
~60x the latency (7.8-9.4 s against ~130 ms) with ~97% of generated tokens
spent inside `reasoning_content`. For a decision worker picking among
3-4 candidate labels, the reasoning budget is pure tax: the task's
information is in the phrasing, not in multi-step derivation.

Two honest notes: this llama.cpp build exposes no reasoning-token counter,
so the share is estimated from character length (recorded in the vote
files); and the negative result is scoped to *this* task family and prompt
- reasoning may still pay for tasks the standard prompt demonstrably fails
(none are in the current suites).

The votes are in `assets/votes/` with provenance; the tool grew
`--thinking`/`--max-tokens` plus reasoning metadata
(`tools/llm_decision_check.py`). The tiering conclusion now covers four
axes measured, not assumed: strength (162), breadth (164), confidence
semantics (165), and now reasoning budget - one of four bought anything
for this workload, and which one it is depends on the family.
