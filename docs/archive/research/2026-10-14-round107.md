# Research Round 107 — Delta replication: 1.61 GB becomes 105 MB

Date: 2026-10-14

## The question

Round 106 made moving an artifact fast (8.8x). It did nothing about *how
much* moves. A lineage that is a fine-tune of its parent shares almost all of
its weights, so sending 1.61 GB to replicate it is the wrong number. This
round implements the blueprint's Phase 5: content-defined chunking with a
content-addressed chunk store, so a replica fetches only what it lacks.

## How much actually differs

Two real lineages, `laya-en.onnx` and `laya-rlcd-v7.onnx` (the RLCD
fine-tune from rounds 73-81), same file sizes:

```text
1 MiB weight blocks: 1608 total, 104 differing (6.5%), spread over blocks 0..836
differing bytes     : ~104 MiB of 1608 MiB
```

So ~105 MB is the honest floor for this pair. The design target was to
transfer approximately that, and nothing else.

## What was built

- **Content-defined chunking** (`chunk_spans`): boundaries are chosen by
  hashing each 64-byte block and cutting where the low bits are zero, with
  min/max bounds. A same-length edit is confined to the chunks that overlap
  it and everything after re-synchronises.
- **Manifest v2** (`build_manifest_cdc`): the v1 shape plus `chunking` and
  per-chunk sizes, still a signed canonical-JSON manifest with per-file
  Merkle roots, still chained by generation and parent hash.
- **Content-addressed store** (`ChunkStore`): chunks live under
  `chunks/<hash[:2]>/<hash>`, so a replica that already holds the parent
  reuses every chunk the child still uses.
- **Fetch only what is missing**: `POST /spore/blobs {"hashes": [...]}`
  returns exactly the requested chunks, batched, over the keep-alive client
  from round 106. Every chunk is still hash-verified before it is written,
  with a per-chunk fallback.

## Result

`tools/spore_delta_check.py` on the real pair, byte-identical reconstruction
verified with `artifact_digest`:

| leg | bytes | transfer | assemble | chunks |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| cold, batched (round 106) | 1610.5 MB | 5.56 s | 4.65 s | 5207 pulled |
| warm, delta (store holds the parent) | **105.0 MB** | **0.369 s** | 45.76 s | 337 pulled, 4870 reused |

**15.3x less data, and 15x less time spent transferring** (5.56 s -> 0.37 s).
The prediction from the diff census was ~105 MB and the delta moved 105.01 MB,
so the chunking is moving essentially only the bytes that changed.

It also speeds up the *preparation*: content-defined manifests for a 1.7 GB
artifact took 339 s in the first (pure-Python, per-byte rolling hash)
implementation and 8.2 s once the block hashing was vectorised - 41x.

## The honest catch

The warm leg is **slower in wall time** (46.3 s versus 10.4 s), and the
breakdown says exactly why: assembling the file re-reads 4,870 chunks from
the on-disk store as individual small files. Delta transfer is a *network*
win and a *local I/O* problem.

That is the right trade only when the link is the scarce resource, which is
the normal case for the goal: over a 100 Mbit/s link the cold leg's 1610 MB
is ~129 s and the warm leg's 105 MB is ~8.4 s, so the delta wins by ~15x
there. On loopback - where the link is nearly free - the local store layout
dominates and the delta looks like a loss.

The fix is not subtle and is not done: pack the chunk store (one append-only
blob plus an index, or an in-memory cache for the duration of a pull) instead
of thousands of small files. Until then the delta's wall-clock benefit only
appears on a real link.

## Verification

`test_content_defined_delta_transfer` (in the 17-test smoke suite, CI):
a 2 MB payload with a 4 KiB region replaced keeps more than half its chunks,
the warm pull reuses exactly that many, the delta sends less than half the
artifact, and both cold and warm reconstructions match the source digest.
The batched-transfer test from round 106, the spore chain, DAG and root
surface checks all still pass - v1 manifests are untouched and take the old
path, so nothing existing changed behaviour.

## Open

- **packed chunk store** - the wall-clock fix named above.
- **wiring into replication**: `rootd --serve-spore` and `join --spore`
  still use v1 fixed-size manifests, so the live reproduction loop does not
  yet benefit from deltas; the library and the measurement do.
- **garbage collection**: nothing ever removes chunks, so a replica's store
  grows with every lineage it has ever seen.
- **granularity trade-off** is measured but not tuned: 256 KiB chunks gave
  5207 chunks and boundaries that resynchronise well for weight blobs, but a
  *insertion* still shifts alignment until the next cut point (measured: only
  3/16 chunks survive a 100-byte insertion, versus 15/16 for a substitution).
