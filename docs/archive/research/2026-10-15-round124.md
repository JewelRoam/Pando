# Research Round 124 — The lineage rule, on the wire

Date: 2026-10-15

## What replay could not show

Round 121 added `count_lineages` and verified it by replaying recorded votes
through the real aggregator. That proves the arithmetic and nothing else: it
cannot show that the flag reaches the coordinator, that the quorum picks the
stems it thinks it picked, that same-lineage votes collapse on the live path,
or that the answer tells an operator which opinions stood behind it.

`tools/lineage_quorum_e2e_check.py` closes that gap. It starts real roots on
loopback, registers real HTTP stems into them, and asks the same question of
each - the same harness style as the end-to-end smoke case, using the
project's deterministic backend so the split is exact.

## Four scenarios, measured

| scenario (stems: 2x `english`, 1x `typed-decisions`) | tier | choice | agreement | stems | lineages |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| flag off - a 2:1 stem majority | `auto` | `approve` | 0.67 | 3 | - |
| flag on - the same quorum | **`verify`** | none | 0.50 | 3 | 2 |
| flag on - 3x `english` replicas | `auto` | `approve` | **1.00** | 3 | **1** |
| flag on + `corroboration_floor = 2` - 3x `english` | **`verify`** | `approve` | 1.00 | 3 | 1 |

The first two rows are the rule working on the wire: a quorum that is a 2:1
stem majority of one lineage over another is an `auto` today, and with the
flag it is a 1:1 tie between lineages, which is `verify` with no choice - a
swarm with two opinions in it and no majority has not decided anything.

## What the third row exposed

Three copies of one lineage reach `auto` with **agreement 1.00**, because
that is what replicas do: they agree perfectly and always will. Round 119
measured a same-lineage pair deciding identically on 240 of 240 tasks, so
"agreement 1.00" here is one opinion corroborated by nothing - and it is
exactly the reading a swarm that grows by copying itself will converge on.
Growth would manufacture confidence.

That is a policy question, not a bug, and the project has kept it open on
purpose since round 98's lone-survivor note: a single device has to be able
to work alone. This round adds the knob instead of changing the default.
`[gating] corroboration_floor` (default 1) refuses `auto` when fewer than
that many distinct lineages voted; the fourth row shows the same replicas
landing on `verify` at floor 2, with the choice still returned the way the
gate's existing `verify` tier does.

The decision response now reports `lineages` whenever it can be observed
(lineage counting on, or a floor above 1) and `corroboration_floor` when it
is set, so an operator can see a lone-opinion quorum instead of inferring it.
The floor is deliberately independent of `count_lineages`: a playbook may
want two opinions without wanting same-lineage votes collapsed.

## What is now guaranteed, and what is not

Guaranteed by the live path, over real HTTP:

- collapsing puts same-lineage stems behind one vote and turns a
  cross-lineage 1:1 split into `verify`;
- `corroboration_floor` > 1 stops any quorum with fewer distinct opinions
  from auto-executing;
- both are visible in the response.

Not established here: the stems are the deterministic protocol backend, so
this proves the *policy and the wire*, not model behaviour on a device. The
model-level claim remains round 121's replay, and the two together are what
the change rests on.
