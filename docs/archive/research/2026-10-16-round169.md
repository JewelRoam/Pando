# Research Round 169 — The student climbs: mixing helps, the incumbent holds

Date: 2026-10-16

Round 168's deflation had a named knob (mix the distillation corpus with
the human-labeled 720) and this round turned it. ft-v6 = the same recipe
as ft-v5 (unfrozen, 1 epoch, seed 5) over the **mixed corpus** (827
distill + 720 human-labeled = 1547 trainer rows).

| member | holdout (teacher labels) | D | E | F |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| english (floor) | 0.8352 | 0.7292 | 0.8375 | 0.8417 |
| ft-v3 (incumbent) | 0.9451 | **0.9417** | **0.9917** | 0.8667 |
| ft-v5 (distill only) | 1.0000 | 0.9208 | 0.8708 | 0.8500 |
| **ft-v6 (mixed)** | 1.0000 | 0.9208 | 0.9083 | 0.8917 |

**Mixing moved the ladder in the predicted direction**: E +3.8 points, F
+4.2 over ft-v5, D unchanged, holdout fidelity intact. Every generation
now beats the floor tier everywhere; none yet beats the incumbent on the
incumbent's home splits. Admission refuses again (the capability floor is
best-seated + 4 points on unseen wording; ft-v6 is +2.5 on F and behind on
D/E).

Two readings for the next turn. First, *under-training* is the cheap
suspect: ft-v3's recipe saw its 720 rows with more passes than one epoch
of a twice-larger mixture; ft-v7 (mixed, 2 epochs, same seed) is already
training, prediction recorded before the run (`metrics/lan-check/ft6-prediction.txt`
holds the ft-v6 prediction; the ft-v7 bet is D/E/F all up, holdout at or
below 1.0). Second, the labels: the dual-teacher filter keeps 88% of G,
but the surviving labels are the *teachers'* opinions, not ground truth -
for splits like E the incumbent's humans still know better, and the loop
should not expect distillation alone to close that.
