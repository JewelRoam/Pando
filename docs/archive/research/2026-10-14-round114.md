# Research Round 114 — Growing by reproduction, and where growth stops helping

Date: 2026-10-14

## Two follow-ups from round 113

Round 113 closed the elasticity loop with a supervisor whose `grow` callback
was a local launcher, and left two things open: wire growth to the real
reproduction path, and give the supervisor memory, because a change takes
time to land.

## Memory

`ElasticSupervisor(cooldown_s=...)`: an action that has actually been executed
starts a cooldown, and the supervisor returns `hold: cooling down Ns after
grow` until it expires. That check exists because a reproduced node must
transfer an artifact, start and register before it can vote - tens of seconds
during which the swarm's latency has not changed yet.

Fixing it surfaced a defect worth naming: the *first* version recorded the
action and started the cooldown even when no executor was configured for it.
In the growth check, which wires only `grow`, the supervisor reported the same
`retire slow-0` every cooldown cycle having never retired anything:

```text
round 3: retire: slow-0 at 365 ms while 3 members are live
round 6: retire: slow-0 at 365 ms while 3 members are live
```

A decision nobody executed is a proposal, not an action: it now neither starts
a cooldown nor appears in `supervisor.actions`.

## Growth through real reproduction

`tools/elastic_growth_check.py` wires `grow` to `pandosi.reproduce` with the
whole chain from earlier rounds: a root serving a content-defined spore, a
signed playbook declaring the lineage and its artifact digest, attestation,
and the `reproduce` grant. The joined node is a real ONNX worker.

Two slow serialized mocks (120 ms) start the swarm; the target is 80 ms; the
member ceiling is 4.

```text
round 0: p50= 449.7 ms members=2 k=1 -> grow: 367 ms > 88 ms and the quorum is
         at its floor (1) with 2/4 members; grow -> reproduced-1 registered=True
round 1: p50= 133.5 ms members=3 k=1 -> hold: cooling down 11s after grow
round 2: p50= 145.7 ms members=3 k=1 -> hold: cooling down 3s after grow
round 3: p50= 119.3 ms members=3 k=1 -> hold: 70 ms within 80 ms target (x1.1)
round 4: p50= 138.7 ms members=3 k=1 -> grow: 96 ms > 88 ms ...;
         grow -> reproduced-2 registered=True
round 5: p50= 981.1 ms members=4 k=1 -> hold: cooling down 8s after grow
round 6: p50= 804.6 ms members=4 k=1 -> hold: 482 ms and at the member ceiling
         (4/4); this is a per-decision-cost problem, not a capacity one
round 7: p50= 791.8 ms members=4 k=1 -> hold: ... at the member ceiling
```

The mechanism works: twice, the swarm decided it was too slow, and twice a
spore was actually transferred, attested and admitted, with the new node
registering as a working member.

## The finding: growth helped once, then hurt badly

The first reproduction took p50 from 449.7 ms to 119.3 ms - the swarm was
right to grow, and the cooldown then correctly waited for the effect.

The second took it from 138.7 ms to **981 ms**, and it stayed at 750-800 ms.

That is not a bug in the loop; it is the loop measuring something true. The
joined nodes are ONNX workers on the *same host*, so each new member competes
for the same CPU - and two CPU-bound members share one core budget while
still counting as two voters. Round 94 found the same thing one layer down
(staging across a shared CPU buys nothing) and round 97 found it in the
pipeline; here it appears as a scaling policy.

The supervisor's own next words are the honest end of the story: at the member
ceiling it reported *"482 ms and at the member ceiling (4/4); this is a
per-decision-cost problem, not a capacity one"*. The signal that triggered
growth also says when growth cannot help.

## What this establishes

```text
elastic growth across devices   -> adds capacity (rounds 105-108, and the
                                   sleep-based members in round 113)
elastic growth within one host  -> adds voters, not cores; measured 7x worse
                                   p50 at 4 members
```

The ceiling is therefore load-bearing in a single-host deployment, and the
policy needs one more input it does not have: whether a candidate member
brings capacity the swarm lacks, or just another process on a machine that is
already busy. `Member` carries latency and checkpoint - it does not carry
anything about the host it lives on.

## Verification

19 smoke tests, including a new assertion that an executed action starts a
cooldown and an unexecuted one does neither. `elastic_loop_check` (sleep-based
members, where growth helps) and `retirement_check` both still pass, and they
show the two halves of elasticity behaving differently for a reason that is
now measured rather than assumed.

## Open

- **host awareness**: growth decisions should know whether a candidate member
  would share hardware with an existing one. The reproduction path already
  knows the target (`Target.host`), so the signal exists; the supervisor does
  not consume it.
- **no rollback**: the second growth made things worse and nothing undid it -
  retirement could, but it requires an operator grant, so an unattended swarm
  can dig itself into a hole it cannot climb out of.
- the check caps members at 4; without a ceiling and without host awareness,
  the trigger (rising latency) would keep asking for more members.
