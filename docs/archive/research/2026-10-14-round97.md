# Research Round 97 — The speed-proportional split, measured: prediction falsified

Date: 2026-10-14

## What was tested

Round 96 predicted that a split in proportion to device speed would
turn round 94's 0.47x into a win, reaching ~83 req/s against ~73 req/s
for single-device replication on the RTX 3070. This round runs it: stage A
on the 3070 (`CUDAExecutionProvider`), stage B on the Intel CPU, cuts chosen
near the balance point.

| cut | T_a (CUDA) | T_b (CPU) | balance | hop rtt | single rps | staged rps | gain |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1500 | 11.49 | 14.03 | 0.82 | 11.14 | 74.05 | 48.88 | **0.66x** |
| 1600 | 11.71 | 10.07 | 0.86 | 1.89 | 71.67 | 53.07 | **0.74x** |

(`--atol 1e-2`, `--threads 4`, 8 decisions per point, baseline measured at
the same concurrency. `hop rtt` is the median `/ping` round trip in the same
run; it varied 1.9-11.1 ms between runs because it is measured under load.)

**The prediction is falsified.** Staging loses at both cuts, and the ordering
is consistent with round 94: 0.47x for the 50/50 cut, 0.66x and 0.74x as the
cut approaches balance. The trend is real and directionally as predicted;
the destination is still below 1.0.

## Two independent reasons it fails

**1. The ceiling is small.** Balancing a `k = 5.9x` slower device against the
fast one gives the fast device `f = T_slow/(T_fast+T_slow) = 0.855` of the
model, so the critical path is `0.855 x 13.7 ms = 11.7 ms` against a
single-device 13.7 ms. The theoretical maximum is therefore **1.17x**, not
the 8.74x round 96 quoted.

**2. The hop is not free under load.** Round 93's premise was `L << T`, based
on a loopback round trip of ~1.2 ms measured idle. Under four concurrent
requests the same hop measured **1.9-11.1 ms** — the same order as a stage.
Because every stage after the first must receive its input across that hop,
the pipeline period is `max(stage_ms + hop)`, not `max(stage_ms)`. At cut
1500 that is `max(11.5, 14.0 + 11.1) = 25.1 ms` -> 40 req/s, and the run
measured 48.9.

The realized period was even a little worse than the pure-hop model
(18.8 ms observed at cut 1600 vs 12.0 ms modeled), which is per-request HTTP
connection setup, activation encode/decode, and single-process contention
between the CUDA session and the CPU stage server.

## Correction to round 96

Round 96's headline comparison — "staging 82.4 req/s vs replication
9.4 req/s, 8.74x" — compared a pipeline against a **forced quorum of 2**
that included the slow device. That baseline was chosen by the planner, not
by the measurement, and it is the worst available option rather than the
best. Measured against the best replication (quorum 1 on the 3070, 74 req/s)
staging is 0.66x. **The 8.74x figure is retracted as a comparison artifact.**

This is exactly the failure mode the round-94 methodology note warned about,
committed one round later in a different disguise.

## Planner fix

`pandosi/topology.py` now encodes what this round measured:

1. `Link.hop_ms` is the per-request hop cost **under load**, separate from
   `rtt_ms`, and it is what the planner prices;
2. the pipeline period is `max(stage_ms + hop)` over all stages, so a hop
   that is comparable to a stage bounds throughput instead of being hidden
   in a latency estimate;
3. the replication baseline evaluates quorum sizes `1..k` and keeps the
   highest-throughput one, because a slow device with the same checkpoint
   adds latency and no information (round 95) - forcing it into the quorum
   is not a baseline, it is a handicap.

With those three changes the planner predicts `replicate` for the round-97
hardware, matching the measurement
(`tools/topology_check.py`, scenario 7). It still predicts `stage` for two
throughput-comparable devices joined by a cheap hop (1.82x) - that case
remains untested on this host and is the last standing claim of the
pipeline line.

## Conclusion so far

For one dominant accelerator plus slow companions, **replicate-and-vote on
the fast device is not a fallback, it is the answer**, and it now has four
independent measurements behind it (rounds 94, 97) rather than a rule of
thumb. The remaining open question is the comparable-device case, which needs
the second GPU.
