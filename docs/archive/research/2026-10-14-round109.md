# Research Round 109 — What actually scales: the quorum, not the replica

Date: 2026-10-14

## The question

Elasticity so far meant membership: nodes can join and leave (rounds 70-71),
and a node can be reproduced onto new hardware (rounds 105-108). But does a
bigger swarm *do more*? That is the question any replica-count policy has to
answer first, and this architecture is not a serving fleet: every decision is
answered by the selected quorum **together**, so one more member is one more
voter on every decision rather than one more worker taking a share.

`tools/scaling_law_check.py` measures it: N replicas with a simulated
per-decision latency, swept over concurrency.

## A measurement mistake worth recording first

The first sweep gave a throughput gain of 0.95-1.03x for 1 -> 5 replicas at
every concurrency, and I nearly wrote "replicas never add capacity". That
number was meaningless: the harness's simulated device was a
`time.sleep()`, which is **infinitely parallel**, so no replica was ever
saturated and the cluster had no capacity limit to scale.

`stemd --serialize` closes a gate around the delay so a replica serves one
decision at a time, like real hardware. Every number below is with
serialization on. The lesson generalises: a mock that cannot be saturated
cannot measure capacity.

## Result A — replicas without a fixed quorum

Default behaviour: the root asks up to `quorum_k = 5`, so the quorum grows
with membership (1, 2, 4, 5 voters for 1, 2, 4, 6 replicas).

| replicas | concurrency | throughput | p50 | quorum |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 13.6 req/s | 73.6 ms | 1 |
| 1 | 4 | 19.6 req/s | 202.7 ms | 1 |
| 6 | 1 | 13.4 req/s | 76.2 ms | 5 |
| 6 | 4 | 21.4 req/s | 179.8 ms | 5 |

Five extra replicas bought **9%** of throughput. Latency under load barely
moved (202.7 -> 179.8 ms), because the queue is formed by the voters, and
every decision occupies all of them.

## Result B — a fixed quorum with rotating subsets

Keep the quorum at 2 and give `pick_quorum` a per-decision rotation within
each checkpoint group (`--quorum-k 2` plus the rotation added this round).
Six replicas can now serve three disjoint pairs:

| replicas | quorum | concurrency | throughput | p50 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | 2 | 1 | 13.5 req/s | 72.7 ms |
| 6 | 2 | 4 | **37.4 req/s** | 102.8 ms |
| 6 | 2 | 8 | **46.5 req/s** | 147.3 ms |

Against result A's 19.6 req/s at concurrency 4, that is **1.9x** - and the
ceiling is visible in the arithmetic: `N / k = 6 / 2 = 3` parallel decisions,
3 x 13.5 = ~40 req/s. Throughput at concurrency 1 is unchanged (13.5 req/s):
a single decision is still a single decision.

## What this establishes

**The scaling unit of PandoSI is the quorum, not the replica.** Membership
growth alone converts into almost nothing; growth plus a fixed quorum size
and subset rotation converts into `N/k`-way parallelism. This also explains
round 70's scale-up result, which showed a quorum of 5 forming and reported
no throughput claim - there was none to report.

It also makes the elasticity policy a *trade* rather than a dial:

```text
larger k  -> more independent voters per decision (agreement, diversity,
             protection against one bad device)
smaller k -> more decisions in parallel from the same hardware
```

Round 95 showed that replicas of the same checkpoint add no information, and
round 99 showed disagreement is where the errors are - so capacity is bought
directly from checking power. A swarm that wants both has to grow `N` *and*
hold `k`, which is exactly why "reproduce more" and "decide faster" are not
the same instruction.

## Changes

- `stemd --delay-ms` / `--serialize`: a mock replica with a device-like
  speed and a serialization gate, so capacity questions are measurable.
- `rootd --quorum-k N`: the quorum size is now a deployment decision rather
  than a constant, with the default unchanged at 5.
- `Coordinator.pick_quorum`: the starting member within each checkpoint group
  rotates per decision, so concurrent decisions can use disjoint subsets.
- `tools/scaling_law_check.py`: the sweep, its reports, and the growth table.

## Verification

17 smoke tests; `swarm_check`, `slow_stem_check`, `remote_latency_check`,
`two_group_check` and `autonomy_check` all still pass with the rotation in
place, so nothing that relied on the previous fixed member selection broke.

## Open

- the rotation is blind: it spreads load without knowing which replicas are
  busy, so a queued voter can still be picked. Real subset scheduling would
  need per-node load, not just latency EWMA.
- `k` is static per deployment; nothing adapts it to current load, which is
  the natural next policy experiment.
- the simulated device is a fixed delay, not a queueing model, so absolute
  numbers are not predictive - only the scaling shape is.
